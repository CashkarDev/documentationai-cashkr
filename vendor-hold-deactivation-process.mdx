## Overview and purpose

The vendor hold and deactivation process sets clear rules for when to temporarily pause or permanently remove a vendor to protect customer experience, service quality, safety, SLAs, and Cashkr’s reputation.

<Callout kind="info">

Use this SOP to decide whether to keep a vendor active, place them on hold, or deactivate them, and to follow the correct approval and documentation flow for each action.

</Callout>

The process helps Cashkr:

- Maintain service quality
- Prevent fraud
- Protect customer safety
- Improve SLA compliance
- Maintain a strong vendor network
- Handle repeat offenders with discipline
- Keep operations clean and trustworthy

This SOP defines when a vendor should be:

- **Put on hold** (temporarily paused)
- **Deactivated** (permanently removed)

---

## 1. Types of vendor statuses

| Status        | Meaning                                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Active**    | Vendor can freely accept and unlock orders                                 |
| **Hold**      | Vendor is temporarily restricted from accepting orders                     |
| **Deactivated** | Vendor is permanently blocked and cannot return unless specially approved |

---

## 2. Who can place vendors on hold or deactivate them

### Vendor Ops team

- Primary owners of vendor discipline.
- Executes hold and deactivation actions after approvals.

### Operations Manager

- Confirms operational issues and SLA violations.

### Vendor Ops Manager

- Provides final approval for **hold** actions.

### Management (CEO/Director)

- Provides final approval for **deactivation** cases.

CX can recommend vendor hold but cannot take action directly.

---

## 3. Reasons to place a vendor on hold

Place a vendor on hold when issues are **moderate but fixable**.

### A. SLA violations (repeated)

- Late pickups
- No call after assignment
- Consistent delays
- High cancellation ratio

If a vendor has **5 or more SLA violations in a week**, place them on hold automatically.

### B. Behaviour issues

- Rude to customers
- Non-cooperative
- Unprofessional conduct
- Miscommunication with Ops or CX

### C. Wrong inspection or improper failures

- Incorrect condition assessment
- Marking wrong failure reasons
- Trying to lower price intentionally

### D. Payment delays (minor)

- Taking too long to complete OTP payment
- Customer complaints about slow payment

### E. Low wallet or low credit issues

If a vendor repeatedly tries to unlock orders without enough credits or wallet balance, keep them on hold until the issue is resolved.

### F. Operational issues

- Not available in their promised area
- Not answering calls repeatedly
- Not completing committed pickups

---

## 4. Reasons for permanent deactivation

Use deactivation for **serious violations** or **repeat offences**.

### A. Fraud or manipulation

Instant deactivation.

Examples:

- Fake UPI screenshot
- Editing payment receipt
- False battery health claims
- Intentional device misreporting
- Attempt to manipulate customer OTP

### B. Customer harassment or safety issue

Any inappropriate behavior towards customers leads to immediate block under safety policy.

### C. Payment scam or theft

If a vendor leaves without paying the customer, deactivate immediately.

### D. Multiple repeated SLA violations

Deactivate if a vendor violates SLAs for **3 or more consecutive weeks**, even after training.

### E. Vendor abandoning orders

If a vendor repeatedly accepts but does not complete orders, deactivate.

### F. Vendor selling or misusing customer data

Any misuse or sale of customer data leads to a permanent ban.

## 5. How to place a vendor on hold

<Steps>

<Step title="Identify the vendor issue" icon="search">

CX, Ops, or Vendor Ops must log:

- Order ID
- Issue type
- Proof such as audio or screenshots

</Step>

<Step title="Review by Vendor Ops" icon="settings">

Vendor Ops reviews:

- SLA reports
- Vendor history
- Past violations and behaviour records

</Step>

<Step title="Apply hold in the admin panel" icon="terminal">

Use the path:

**Admin Panel → Vendors → Vendor Profile → HOLD Vendor**

Fill these fields:

- Reason for hold (mandatory)
- Internal notes
- Date and time

</Step>

<Step title="Notify the vendor (optional)" icon="message-square">

Optionally send a message via WhatsApp or SMS:

`Your account is temporarily on hold due to operational issues. Vendor Ops will contact you.`

</Step>

<Step title="Conduct vendor training call" icon="phone">

Vendor Ops must call the vendor to:

- Explain the issue
- Provide guidance
- Set expectations

Keep the vendor on hold until Vendor Ops or Ops approves reactivation.

</Step>

</Steps>

---

## 6. How to deactivate a vendor

<Steps>

<Step title="Gather complete evidence" icon="folder">

Collect:

- Screenshots
- UPI proof (for fraud or payment issues)
- Customer complaints
- SLA logs
- Audio recordings

</Step>

<Step title="Vendor Ops Manager review" icon="shield">

Vendor Ops Manager reviews the case and confirms the severity and whether it fits deactivation criteria.

</Step>

<Step title="Get management approval" icon="check-circle">

CEO or Director reviews the case and must approve deactivation before any action in the admin panel.

</Step>

<Step title="Deactivate in the admin panel" icon="terminal">

Use the path:

**Admin Panel → Vendors → Vendor Profile → DEACTIVATE Vendor**

Fill these fields:

- Reason
- Internal notes
- Evidence attached (mandatory)

</Step>

<Step title="Add vendor to fraud or blacklist registry" icon="database">

Record the vendor permanently in the fraud or blacklist registry for future audit and reference.

</Step>

</Steps>

## 7. Re-activation rules

### Vendor on hold

You may reactivate a vendor on hold when:

- Vendor completes training
- Wallet or credit issue is resolved
- SLA performance improves
- Ops or Vendor Ops approves reactivation

### Deactivated vendor

You cannot reactivate a deactivated vendor unless:

- Management gives special approval
- Fraud investigation is cleared
- Vendor is retrained thoroughly
- Compliance check is passed

In general, **deactivation equals a permanent ban**.

---

## 8. BD team responsibilities

The BD team uses vendor status data to:

- Identify coverage gaps created by held or deactivated vendors
- Suggest onboarding of new vendors
- Highlight areas where vendor shortages affect SLA performance
- Track impact on city performance
- Coordinate with Vendor Ops to refill vendor supply

The BD team does not place vendors on hold or deactivate them but ensures coverage after disciplinary actions.

---

## 9. Customer experience protocol during vendor issues

When a vendor is held or deactivated, CX must:

1. Apologize to the customer
2. Explain that the vendor is being reassigned
3. Immediately coordinate with Ops to assign a new vendor
4. Follow up until order completion

The goal is that the customer should never feel the impact of internal vendor issues.

---

## 10. Internal documentation requirements

<Callout kind="info">

Every hold or deactivation action must have complete documentation. If documentation is missing, treat the action as invalid and correct it.

</Callout>

For every **hold** or **deactivation**, record:

- Detailed reason
- Supporting evidence
- Date and time of action
- Who approved it
- SLA count (if applicable)
- Related order IDs

## 11. One-page quick summary (training card)

### When to hold a vendor

- SLA delays or high weekly SLA violations
- Wrong failure or inspection issues
- Minor payment delays
- Behaviour issues
- Low wallet or low credits
- Unreachable or not completing pickups

### When to deactivate a vendor

- Fraud or manipulation
- Payment scam or theft
- Customer harassment or safety issues
- Multiple SLA violations across consecutive weeks
- Fake screenshots or edited proofs
- Any serious safety or trust issue

### Teams and roles

- **Vendor Ops:** Executes holds and deactivations
- **Ops:** Confirms issues and SLA violations
- **CX:** Reports issues and handles customer communication
- **BD:** Ensures coverage replacement after actions
- **Management:** Approves deactivation cases
