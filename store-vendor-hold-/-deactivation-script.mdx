## Overview

**Used by:** Store Partnerships / Vendor Management Team  

**Purpose:**  
Define when and how a store vendor should be placed on Hold or Deactivated status, including reasons, actions, communication format, and process flow.

**Objective:**

- Ensure consistent, fair, and transparent action on vendor compliance issues.
- Distinguish correctly between temporary Hold and permanent Deactivation.
- Maintain smooth communication to vendors without conflict or confusion.

## Training script (for vendor managers)

<Steps>

<Step title="1. Understanding the two actions" icon="info">

Hold and Deactivation are two distinct actions with different meanings and impact.

| Action       | Meaning                                    | Impact                                                                 |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hold         | Temporary pause on vendor activity         | Vendor cannot create or complete new orders; existing store orders remain visible |
| Deactivation | Permanent removal from the store network   | All app access revoked, Wallet/Credit settled, and account closed     |

> A Hold is for correction.  
> A Deactivation is for closure.

<Callout kind="info">

Hold is used for temporary correction. Deactivation is used for permanent closure when the vendor cannot be trusted for reinstatement or refuses to comply.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step title="2. Reasons for placing a vendor on Hold" icon="alert-triangle">

Use a Hold when you expect the vendor to correct an issue and continue in the network.

| Category               | Example                                             | Action                     |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| SLA breach             | Repeated delay in order completion or device collection | Hold for 24–72 hours       |
| Unprofessional behavior| Rude conduct with store or customer                | Immediate Hold             |
| Low activity           | No store orders handled for 7+ days                | Soft Hold until confirmed active |
| Wallet/Credit default  | Daily credit settlement not completed              | Hold until dues cleared    |
| Missing reports        | 3+ days of skipped daily reporting                 | Hold until reporting resumed |
| Ongoing dispute / audit| Refund or reversal under investigation             | Hold until issue resolved  |

</Step>

<Step title="3. Hold process flow" icon="list-ordered">

Follow this sequence whenever you place a vendor on Hold.

| Step                 | Action                                        | Owner                  |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 1. Violation detected| SLA, Ops, or Store feedback triggers flag     | Vendor Management Team |
| 2. Verification      | Confirm issue from system logs or store records | Ops Associate          |
| 3. Approval          | Ops Manager authorizes Hold                   | Ops Manager            |
| 4. Status change     | Set vendor to Hold in Admin Panel             | Vendor Management      |
| 5. Communication     | Inform vendor officially                      | Vendor Support Team    |

</Step>

<Step title="4. Communication template — Hold notice" icon="mail">

Use this template when informing a vendor about a Hold.

```text
Subject: Temporary Hold on Your Cashkr Vendor Account

Hi [Vendor Name],

Your account has been temporarily placed on Hold due to [Reason – e.g., missed SLA compliance or pending daily credit settlement].

During this Hold period:
• You will not be able to create or complete new store orders.
• Ongoing orders remain visible for reconciliation.

Once the issue is reviewed and resolved, your account may be reactivated.

Please reply confirming when the pending requirement is completed.

— Cashkr Vendor Management Team
```

</Step>

<Step title="5. Hold duration and reactivation" icon="clock">

Align Hold duration and reactivation strictly with the issue type.

| Issue type           | Typical Hold duration | Reactivation trigger               |
| -------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| SLA breach / delay   | 48–72 hours           | After 3 days of compliance review  |
| Credit default       | Until dues cleared    | Once daily payment completed       |
| Reporting gaps       | 48 hours              | After daily summaries resumed      |
| Audit / fraud review | Until audit complete  | After Ops Manager approval         |

> Once Hold is cleared, vendor must log out and log in again to restore access.

<Callout kind="tip">

After you clear the Hold, remind the vendor to log out and log in again so their access reflects the updated status.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step title="6. Reasons for permanent deactivation" icon="x-circle">

Use Deactivation only for serious or repeated violations where reinstatement is not expected.

| Category                | Description                                         | Severity |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Fraudulent activity     | Fake order marking, fake ID, duplicate payment claims | Critical |
| Repeated misconduct     | 3+ complaints from store or customer               | High     |
| Credit or payment default| Failure to settle daily credits repeatedly        | High     |
| Repeated SLA failures   | SLA below 70% for 3 consecutive weeks              | Medium   |
| Policy violation        | Breach of contract terms or data misuse            | High     |
| Inactive more than 30 days | Vendor inactive without intimation              | Low      |

> Permanent deactivation is used only when the vendor cannot be trusted for reinstatement or refuses to comply.

</Step>

<Step title="7. Deactivation process flow" icon="settings">

Follow this sequence for every permanent deactivation.

| Step             | Action                                     | Owner           |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------- |
| 1. Record review | Verify vendor history, violations, dues    | Vendor Manager  |
| 2. Final approval| Confirm by Ops Head                        | Ops Head        |
| 3. Settlement    | Reconcile and close Wallet + Credits       | Finance         |
| 4. Deactivate    | Remove access in Admin Panel               | Vendor Management |
| 5. Inform vendor | Send final closure email                   | Vendor Support  |

</Step>

<Step title="8. Communication template — Deactivation notice" icon="mail">

Use this template for permanent deactivation communication.

```text
Subject: Account Deactivation Notice – Cashkr Store Vendor Program

Hi [Vendor Name],

After repeated review and non-compliance with Cashkr’s vendor policies, your account has been deactivated permanently.

Reason: [Specify issue clearly — e.g., repeated SLA breach, unprofessional behavior, or unpaid dues].

Your Wallet and Credit balance (if any) will be settled as per policy within 5 business days.

You will no longer have access to the Cashkr Vendor App or related systems.

Thank you for your past service.

— Cashkr Vendor Management Team
```

</Step>

<Step title="9. Reinstatement rules" icon="shield">

Apply these rules consistently when vendors ask about reinstatement.

> Once deactivated, reactivation is not guaranteed.

> Vendors removed for performance or inactivity can reapply after 30 days through a new onboarding review.

> Vendors deactivated for fraud, misconduct, or unpaid dues are permanently blacklisted.

</Step>

<Step title="10. Reporting and documentation" icon="bar-chart">

Log every Hold and Deactivation and keep reports updated.

| Task                     | Frequency | Owner              |
| ------------------------ | --------- | ------------------ |
| Hold / Deactivation log  | Real-time | Vendor Management  |
| Weekly status summary    | Weekly    | Ops Associate      |
| Pending review list      | Daily     | Ops Manager        |
| Audit of actions         | Monthly   | Compliance         |

> Every Hold or Deactivation must include:  
> • Vendor Name and ID  
> • Reason category  
> • Supporting proof  
> • Action date and approver name

</Step>

</Steps>

## Quick reference summary

Use this table as a one-glance reference during calls.

| Type         | Trigger                                         | Duration     | Access   | Reactivation                     |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------- | -------------------------------- |
| Hold         | Temporary issues (SLA, pending dues, missing reports) | 24–72 hours | Limited  | Allowed after review             |
| Deactivation | Repeated or serious violations                  | Permanent   | Blocked  | Not allowed (except inactive vendors) |

## Key talking points

<Callout kind="alert">

No manual settlements during Hold or Deactivation.  
Deactivation is final only after financial clearance.

</Callout>

- Hold equals temporary pause, not punishment.
- All Hold and Deactivation actions must be logged and approved.
- Communication tone must stay factual and professional.
- No manual settlements during Hold or Deactivation.
- Deactivation is final only after financial clearance.
