## Overview

The No Action page in Orders - B2C shows orders that are assigned to a vendor but have not progressed because the vendor has not taken any recorded action.

<Callout kind="info">
  An order qualifies as No Action when it is assigned to a vendor, but the vendor has not started the pickup process or updated the order status after assignment.
</Callout>

Admins use this view to spot stalled orders quickly, follow up with vendors, and keep customer pickups moving.

The count badge next to No Action in the sidebar shows the real-time number of orders in this state and should be checked daily.

## What the No Action list shows

Each row in the No Action list represents one order that is assigned to a vendor but idle.

- **Created At** — Date and time the order was created.
- **Model** — Device model the customer wants to sell.
- **City** — City where the pickup is scheduled.
- **Type** — Device category such as Mobile, Laptop, Tablet, or Mac.
- **Status** — Current status of the order. It shows Assigned because the vendor has been assigned but has not acted.

## Why orders appear in No Action

Orders move into No Action when vendor activity is missing after assignment.

- The order has been assigned to a vendor.
- The vendor has not started the pickup, updated the order status, or taken any recorded action within the expected timeframe.
- The vendor may have accepted the order but gone inactive or unresponsive.
- The vendor may have forgotten about the order or deprioritised it.

## Difference between No Action and Delayed

No Action and Delayed are related but focus on different problems.

- **No Action** — The order is assigned to a vendor, but the vendor has not done anything with it yet. The focus is on vendor inactivity after assignment.
- **Delayed** — The order has crossed its scheduled pickup date without being completed. The focus is on missed pickup timelines.

An order can appear in both No Action and Delayed if the vendor never acted and the pickup date has also passed.

## Filters and controls

Use filters at the top of the No Action page to narrow down the list and focus on the most important stalled orders.

- **Device Type Filter** — Filter orders by device category such as Mobile, Laptop, Tablet, or Mac.
- **City Filter** — Filter orders by the city where the pickup is scheduled.
- **From Date and To Date** — Filter orders by their creation date range to focus on older or newer stalled orders.
- **Show Orders with Test Accounts** — Include or exclude orders created by test accounts during testing or QA.
- **Search** — Search for a specific order by order details, device name, or customer information.
- **Reset and Apply Filters** — Click Apply Filters to apply the selected filters. Click Reset to clear all filters and return to the full list.
- **Pagination** — Use Previous and Next to move between pages. The total result count appears below the table.

<Callout kind="alert">
  During normal operations, keep test account orders excluded so you focus on real customer orders. Enable Show Orders with Test Accounts only when you intentionally review test data.
</Callout>

## Order flow for No Action orders

No Action sits in the middle of the overall order lifecycle and highlights where vendor activity is missing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Order created and assigned to vendor" icon="download">
    The customer creates an order, and an admin or system assigns it to a vendor for pickup.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Vendor receives order in vendor app" icon="monitor">
    The assigned vendor sees the order in their vendor app but does not start the pickup or update the status.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Order crosses inactivity threshold" icon="alert-triangle">
    Because the vendor has not taken any recorded action after assignment, the order appears in the No Action list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Admin follows up with vendor" icon="phone">
    An admin reviews the No Action list, checks how long the order has been idle, and calls the vendor to prompt action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Vendor acts or admin reassigns" icon="settings">
    When the vendor finally acts on the order, it moves to In Progress. If the vendor remains unresponsive, the admin can reassign the order to another available vendor in the same pincode, and the reassigned order then moves to In Progress under the new vendor.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Admin actions on No Action orders

Use the No Action view as a daily work queue to unblock stalled orders.

- Open the order detail and check when it was assigned and how long it has been idle.
- Call the assigned vendor directly to prompt them to act on the order.
- If the vendor is unresponsive or unable to complete the pickup, reassign the order to another available vendor in the same pincode.
- Contact the customer to update them on the status and reassure them the pickup is being arranged.
- Escalate to the operations manager if a vendor repeatedly has orders in No Action.

## Admin responsibilities and best practices

Admins own the health of the No Action queue and vendor behaviour linked to it.

- Check the No Action page daily and prioritise orders that have been idle the longest.
- Call vendors with stalled orders and push them to take action immediately.
- Reassign orders where the vendor is unreachable or consistently inactive.
- Use the count badge in the sidebar as a quick health indicator. A growing No Action count signals a vendor activity problem.
- Cross-reference with the Vendors - B2C page to check the vendor's overall In Progress count and trust score before reassigning.
- If a vendor has multiple orders in No Action regularly, consider reviewing their trust score and Max Leads limit.

<Callout kind="alert">
  Treat any order that remains in No Action for more than 24 hours as a priority. Contact the vendor immediately and take reassignment or escalation decisions to protect the customer experience.
</Callout>

## Connections to other modules

No Action ties closely to other views that track order progress and vendor performance.

- **In Progress** — Once the vendor takes action on the order, it moves to In Progress.
- **Vendors - B2C** — Vendor activity and trust score are directly linked to how often their orders appear in No Action.
- **Not Assigned** — If an order is reassigned and the new vendor also takes no action, it may cycle back here from Not Assigned.
- **Delayed** — Orders that remain in No Action past their pickup date will also appear in Delayed.
- **Quality - B2C** — Repeated No Action orders from a vendor can negatively affect their quality metrics and trust grade.

## Critical operational notes

<Callout kind="alert">
  An order in No Action is still assigned to a vendor, the customer's device has not been picked up, and the order has not progressed. Delays here directly impact the customer experience, so keep this section under close watch.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="alert">
  When you reassign an order from one vendor to another, the order leaves the original vendor's queue and becomes available to the newly assigned vendor. Inform the original vendor whenever you reassign their order to maintain accountability and avoid confusion.
</Callout>
