## Overview

Use this SOP when handling SLA issues for **Cashkr vendors, Ops team, CX, and Support**. It defines what counts as an SLA violation, how violations are tracked, when escalation triggers, and what actions the Ops team must take at each stage.

The goal is to maintain a consistent customer experience and remove poor-performing or high-risk vendors while treating everyone fairly and consistently.

<Callout kind="info">

Two key rules:

- 5+ violations in a week automatically trigger escalation.
- Fraud and high-risk violations are **zero tolerance** and lead to an immediate block.

</Callout>

### Purpose

This SOP defines:

- What counts as an SLA violation
- How violations are tracked
- How many violations trigger escalation
- What actions the Ops Team should take
- When vendors are fined, put on hold, or terminated

## What is SLA?

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) is the set of rules every vendor must follow to ensure:

- On-time pickups
- Accurate inspections
- Professional behaviour
- Proper payment flow
- Zero fraud
- Completed orders within the expected time

Breaking these rules counts as an **SLA violation**.

## Types of SLA violations

There are six major SLA categories, each with defined violation types.

<Tabs>

## SLA violation severity levels

SLA violations fall into three severity categories. Use these levels to decide the default response before checking the escalation rules.

| Level                     | Examples                                             | Default action                                      |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Level 1 — Minor           | Late by a small margin, customer not responding, low-impact issues | Warning and education.                              |
| Level 2 — Major           | No-show, wrong inspection, rude behaviour, offline payment attempt | Credit deduction, lead limit reduction, SLA strike. |
| Level 3 — Critical / Fraud | Taking device without payment, fake entries, intentional cheating, threatening customer | Immediate hold or block, escalation to Ops Manager, possible legal action. |

## SLA violation tracking system

Monitor every vendor on a **weekly** basis using:

- App data
- `To Be Failed` orders
- Customer complaints
- Ops feedback
- Payment logs

If a vendor exceeds **5 violations in a week**, escalation **automatically triggers** based on the escalation ladder below.

## Escalation rules

Use this escalation ladder when weekly violations or specific behaviours meet the triggers.

<Steps>

<Step title="Stage 1 — Warning (1–2 violations/week)" icon="info">

**Trigger**

- Vendor has **1–2 violations in a week**.

**Ops team actions**

- Call the vendor.
- Explain the violation clearly.
- Provide behaviour and process correction.
- Reset expectations on SLA and customer experience.

**Vendor status**

- **Active** (no restrictions).

</Step>

<Step title="Stage 2 — Strike & training (3–5 violations/week)" icon="alert-triangle">

**Trigger**

- Vendor has **3–5 violations in a week**.

**Ops team actions**

- Issue an **SLA Strike**.
- Schedule mandatory training on pickup and behaviour.
- Reduce daily unlock limit.
- Monitor performance closely for the next 7 days.

**Vendor status**

- **Active with restrictions** (reduced limits and close monitoring).

</Step>

<Step title="Stage 3 — Hold (5+ violations/week)" icon="x-circle">

**Trigger**

- Vendor has **5+ violations in a week** (as per your company rule for holds).

**Ops team actions**

- Put the vendor on **temporary hold**.
- Stop all new orders for the vendor.
- Deduct credits for vendor faults.
- Inform the vendor on WhatsApp or SMS.
- Require the vendor to complete retraining before reactivation.

**Vendor status**

- **On Hold** (no new orders until retraining and approval).

</Step>

<Step title="Stage 4 — Escalation to Ops Manager" icon="chevron-right">

**Trigger**

- Vendor crosses **5+ violations in a week**, or
- Vendor has multiple holds within 30 days, or
- There are customer escalations, behaviour issues, or payment misconduct.

**Ops Manager actions**

- Review the entire vendor performance and history.
- Decide on fine, limit reduction, or long-term hold.
- If the issue repeats after action, move the vendor to Stage 5.

**Vendor status**

- **Critical review** (under Ops Manager assessment).

</Step>

<Step title="Stage 5 — Permanent block / termination" icon="trash">

**Trigger**

- Repeated major violations, fraud attempts, payment manipulation, customer harassment, or device stealing / unpaid pickups.

**Ops Manager + CEO actions**

- Permanently block the vendor.
- Remove area access.
- Flag the vendor in the system to avoid re-onboarding.
- Escalate legally if required.

**Vendor status**

- **Terminated** (no future work with Cashkr).

</Step>

</Steps>

## Internal Ops rules summary

### Always deduct credits when

- Vendor fault
- Wrong inspection
- No-show
- Offline payment
- Behaviour issue

### Always refund credits when

- Customer fault
- Mismatch created by customer
- Customer cancels
- OTP issues from customer side
