Used by: CX Executives / Support Staff

Purpose: standardize how Cashkr’s CX team acknowledges, logs, and escalates customer complaints or feedback — ensuring professional, calm, and trackable resolution.

## Quick checklist

Use this checklist live on call or chat to stay on track:

- Acknowledge the issue with empathy and stay calm.
- Capture key details: what happened, when, order or device details.
- Identify and tag the correct complaint category in the CRM.
- Create or confirm the ticket and share the Ticket ID with the customer.
- Route the case to the correct internal team based on the category.
- Set a clear expectation for review and update time (for example, 24 working hours).
- Add internal notes and severity; mark escalation level if needed.
- Follow up with the customer with the outcome or a clear next ETA.

## When this script applies

Use this script whenever the customer is unhappy, wants a senior, or is questioning service quality.

| Type                  | Examples                                                                                     |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 😠 Customer complaint | "Your agent behaved badly" / "Pickup was delayed" / "Payment not received"                  |
| 📩 Escalation request | "Connect me to your senior / CEO / manager"                                                 |
| ⚖️ Service issue      | "Device was picked up but not paid for" / "Price was changed unfairly"                      |
| 🧾 Feedback           | "Support team did not help me properly"                                                     |

## Script overview

### A. Acknowledge and stay calm (first response)

Use one of these to open the interaction:

> "I am really sorry for the inconvenience, \[Customer Name].  
> I completely understand how frustrating this must be.  
> Let us get this sorted out — I will make sure your concern is properly reviewed."

> "Thank you for bringing this to our attention. We truly appreciate your patience — I will ensure this is escalated to the right team immediately."

### B. Identify the complaint type (internal tagging)

Tag the complaint correctly so it follows the right internal route.

| Complaint category        | Examples                                 | Escalation route         |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| 💳 Payment related        | "Payment not received"                   | Finance → Ops            |
| 📦 Pickup / delay         | "Agent did not arrive"                   | Vendor Mgmt → Ops        |
| 💬 Behavior / communication | "Agent was rude"                       | CX Lead → HR             |
| 💰 Price / quote dispute  | "Price changed suddenly"                 | Pricing Team             |
| ⚙️ App / technical        | "Unable to place order"                  | Tech Support             |
| 🧾 Refund / return        | "Device taken, not refunded"             | Ops + Finance            |

CX Executive must log the complaint under the correct tag in the CRM or ticket system.

### C. Escalation acknowledgement template (standard message)

Use this once you have logged and escalated the ticket:

> "Hi \[Customer Name],  
>  
> Thank you for sharing your concern. Your complaint has been recorded under Ticket ID: \[####].  
> I have escalated this to our \[Department Name] team for urgent review.  
> You will receive a detailed update within 24 working hours.  
>  
> We truly value your patience and assure you this will be handled with priority."

### D. If customer asks to speak to a senior

Use this when the customer explicitly asks for a senior:

> "I completely understand. I will escalate this to my supervisor right away and ensure a callback or update within 24 hours.  
>  
> Our escalation process ensures your case is reviewed directly by the concerned manager for faster resolution."

CX marks ticket as `Escalation – Customer Requested Supervisor`.

## Handling angry or abusive customers

<Callout kind="alert">

When the customer is angry or abusive, stay calm, protect your own boundaries, and still aim to resolve the issue.

Use this first:

> "I understand your frustration, and I truly want to help.  
> May I request that we continue calmly so I can resolve this for you?"

If abusive language continues:

> "I am here to assist, but I will need to pause the chat or call if this continues.  
> Please allow me to escalate this respectfully to a senior executive."

Tag such cases as `High Severity – Behavioural Case`.

</Callout>

<ul>

</ul>

  </Step>
  <Step title="CX updates customer">

CX shares the final resolution or clear ETA with the customer.

<ul>
  <li><strong>Owner:</strong> CX Executive</li>
  <li><strong>TAT:</strong> Within 24–48 hrs</li>
</ul>

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Final resolution communication (follow-up)

Use this once you receive a decision from the internal team:

> "Hi \[Customer Name],  
>  
> Your complaint (Ticket ID: \[####]) has been reviewed by our \[Department] team.  
>  
> Here is the outcome:  
> • \[Brief resolution summary]  
> • \[If applicable] Refund processed / Pickup rescheduled / Agent replaced  
>  
> Thank you for your patience — we have ensured corrective action has been taken."

If the case is still under review:

> "Your complaint is still under review by our internal team.  
> I will make sure to follow up and get an update for you by \[Next Date/Time]."

## Escalation levels (internal hierarchy)

| Level | Escalated to        | When triggered                                   |
| ----- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| L1    | CX Manager          | Unresolved >24 hrs or high severity             |
| L2    | Ops Manager         | Payment, return, vendor behaviour               |
| L3    | CEO / Director      | Legal or public review threat cases             |

## Tone, behaviour, and key phrases

<Callout kind="tip">

Stay empathetic, accountable, and clear in every interaction.

**Tone and behaviour guidelines**

- ✅ Always thank the customer for reporting an issue.
- ✅ Never argue or justify — acknowledge, then escalate.
- ✅ Avoid generic replies like "We are working on it." Give a clear time or ticket number instead.
- ✅ End every interaction with assurance and accountability.

**Key phrases for brand tone**

- ✅ "We are genuinely sorry for the experience."
- ✅ "I will make sure this is looked into on priority."
- ✅ "You will receive a confirmed update soon."
- ✅ "Your feedback means a lot to us."

</Callout>
