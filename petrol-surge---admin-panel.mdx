## Overview

Petrol Surge configures the trip bonus paid to pickup partners on orders that would otherwise be refused. It is an operator tool: Ops sets the rules, Finance sets the budgets, and both watch the spend.

The bonus has two layers. A **trip allowance** is deterministic and predictable, derived from distance and device dwell time. A **surge** on top is reactive, driven by starvation, weather and time, and is always capped.

## Before anything is configured

Three questions gate the design. None are engineering problems, and two change the numbers materially.

| Question | Why it blocks | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are per-transition order timestamps stored, or is the status column overwritten? | The whole system is fed by one event stream — offer shown → accepted, rejected or expired. If status is overwritten, that history does not exist and nothing here is buildable. | Engineering |
| What is gross margin per completed order, by device band? | Every cap below is anchored to what food and quick-commerce platforms pay, not to what a Cashkr pickup earns. ₹250 is either conservative or ruinous depending on this number. | Finance |
| Does a travel bonus enter the 5% gig-worker contribution base? | The Social Security (Central) Rules 2026, effective 8 May 2026, require aggregators to contribute a notified percentage of turnover or 5% of amounts paid to gig workers. This is the difference between surge costing X and 1.05X. | Yogesh |

## Rule fields

Rules are versioned. Changing a rule creates a new version rather than overwriting the old one, so any past bonus can be explained.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| City | Required. Rules are scoped per city. |
| Zone | Optional. A zone rule overrides the city rule. |
| Free kilometres | Round-trip distance covered before the allowance starts. Placeholder: 6 km. |
| Rate per kilometre | Applied beyond the free radius. Placeholder: ₹6/km, which matches both the internal cost model and the ₹5–10/km the market pays. |
| Dwell allowance | Per device category, covering time on site. Placeholder: phone ₹0, tablet ₹20, laptop ₹40. |
| Step amount | Each escalation level adds this much. Placeholder: ₹20. |
| Max escalation | How many steps the ladder may climb. Placeholder: 3. |
| Absolute cap | Hard ceiling per order. Placeholder: ₹250. |
| Percentage cap | Ceiling as a share of the customer quote. Placeholder: 12%. |
| Trigger flags | Per-trigger enable switches and parameters. |
| Effective from / to | Version validity window. |

<Callout kind="warning">

**The percentage cap is the one people forget.** Without it the system will one day pay ₹180 of bonus on a ₹900 device, and nobody finds out until month-end. Always set both caps.

</Callout>

## Triggers

Six triggers, each independently switchable so one can be disabled without disabling the bonus.

| Trigger | Fires on | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance band | Round-trip km, partner service-area centroid → pickup pincode centroid | Deliberately **not live GPS**. Vendor location awareness is tier-1 only today, and centroids are far harder to spoof. |
| Low order value | Bonus-to-quote ratio, not distance alone | The trigger that protects margin. A ₹1,200 feature phone 12 km out is never worth a trip to anyone. |
| Time of day | Evening slots, weekends | — |
| Weather | Rain | Mumbai monsoon. |
| Zone starvation | Unassigned for T minutes, or rejected N times | Most powerful and most dangerous. It finds the clearing price without anyone guessing it, and it also teaches partners that refusing pays more. See abuse controls. |
| Slot-breach risk | Order approaching its committed pickup window | — |

## Admin workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a rule" icon="plus">
    Configure the bonus for a city, or for a zone within it.

    - Select the **City**, and a **Zone** if the rule is narrower than the city.
    - Set **Free kilometres**, **Rate per kilometre** and the **Dwell allowance** per device category.
    - Set **Step amount** and **Max escalation** for the reactive layer.
    - Set both the **Absolute cap** and the **Percentage cap**.
    - Enable the triggers that should apply, with their parameters.
    - Set the **Effective from** date.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Dry-run before saving" icon="flask-conical">
    Never ship a rule change untested.

    - Point the candidate rule at the last 30 days of real orders.
    - Review projected spend, orders affected and median bonus.
    - Without this step every rule change is a live experiment on production money.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the city budget" icon="banknote">
    Cap what the city can spend in a day.

    - Set the daily cap per city.
    - Consumption is checked atomically before an offer renders, so the cap cannot be overshot by concurrent offers.
    - An alert fires when a city crosses 70% of its daily budget.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Monitor" icon="activity">
    Watch the spend and the behaviour it produces.

    - Review consumption against budget, and the bonus distribution at p95 rather than median.
    - Watch the accept rate on **non-surged** orders as a counter-metric.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Kill it if needed" icon="power">
    A global kill switch disables the bonus everywhere.

    - Exercise it in production at least once before widening the rollout, so it is known to work when it is needed.
    - With the bonus off, orders continue to be offered and assigned normally.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Caps and the exit condition

If the bonus reaches its cap and the order is still unclaimed, the system must stop bidding and route the order elsewhere — batch it with another pickup in the same pincode, push the customer to a later slot with a small quote sweetener, or offer a drop-off at a partner store.

Most surge systems have no exit condition and quietly become an uncapped subsidy. This one must be able to say *no bid at cap* and hand the order to a different fulfilment path.

## Funding

| Option | How it works | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — Direct wallet credit** | Post the bonus into the partner's ledger on completion. | Clean, visible to the agent, straight P&L cost. The per-partner ledger on the pooled HDFC account already supports it. Waits on the rupee-denominated wallet. |
| **B — Lead-fee waiver** | Reduce or waive the credits charged for that order. | Costs foregone revenue rather than cash out, needs no payment integration, ships quickly. But where a partner is a shop employing riders, the money never reaches the person on the bike. |
| **C — Hybrid** *(recommended)* | B for the pilot, A once the rupee wallet lands, with a rule that partner-businesses must surface the bonus to their agent. | Learns price elasticity now without waiting on the HDFC migration. |

Option B's flaw grows over time: the partner growth path actively markets "start solo, then hire your own pickup agents", so an increasing share of trips is run by someone who is not the wallet holder.

## Abuse controls

| Vector | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Collusive rejection to pump the ladder | Cap escalation at three steps. Score partners on rejection rate. Show escalated offers to high-rejection partners later rather than first, and randomise the broadcast order so refusing does not reliably win the higher offer. |
| Accept, then cancel after seeing the address | Forfeit the bonus, apply a cancellation penalty (10% of value, capped), and count it toward the rejection score. |
| Address manipulation to inflate distance | Freeze the pincode at order creation. Any later change triggers a re-quote and resets escalation to zero. |
| Self-dealing — a relative books a far pickup to farm the bonus | Dedupe on customer phone and payout beneficiary; alert on repeat customer↔partner pairs. There are ~15.8k saved beneficiary rows to check against. |
| GPS spoofing | Largely neutralised by pincode centroids — a good reason to keep centroids even after live tracking ships. |
| Runaway spend | Hard daily cap per city checked atomically before the offer renders, plus the global kill switch and the 70% alert. |

## Rollout

<Steps>
  <Step title="Phase 0 — instrumentation only" icon="database">
    Ship the append-only order assignment event table. No surge logic, nothing paid, nothing shown.

    Two weeks of this shows whether travel cost is actually the problem, and sizes the partner-refusal slice of the gap between ~270 orders/day arriving and ~39/day completing. **This phase is worth shipping regardless of whether the bonus follows.**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Phase 1 — shadow mode" icon="eye">
    Run the full calculation on every order for two weeks. Store the result, pay nothing, show nothing. This produces the real budget model before a rupee is spent.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Phase 2 — one zone, allowance only" icon="map-pin">
    Trip allowance live in a single zone, no escalation ladder, behind a per-partner flag.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Phase 3 — add the ladder" icon="trending-up">
    Escalation enabled in the same zone, with every abuse control active from day one.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Phase 4 — expand" icon="map">
    Zone by zone, exercising the kill switch in production before each widening.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Monitoring

<Callout kind="warning">

**Hold out 10–20% of eligible orders, permanently.** Without an unsurged control group you cannot tell orders the bonus *caused* from orders that would have completed anyway, and you will pay for the latter indefinitely.

</Callout>

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Completed orders/day against the ~39/day baseline | The headline, but meaningless without the holdout. |
| Offer→accept rate and time to first accept | Whether the bonus is changing partner behaviour at all. |
| **Cost per incremental completed order** | Spend ÷ completions above the holdout baseline. **This is the number that decides whether the feature lives.** |
| Spend as a share of gross margin | The affordability ceiling. |
| Bonus distribution at p95, not median | The median looks fine long after the tail stops being fine. |
| Accept rate on *non-surged* orders | The counter-metric. If partners learn to wait for a bonus, the baseline rots while spend climbs. |

## Developer and tester notes

**For developers**

- Snapshot every calculation input on the quote record. A partner will dispute a ₹40 bonus six months later and the system must reconstruct exactly what it saw.
- Lock at acceptance; settlement reads the locked amount and never recalculates.
- Redis carries the hot path — zone escalation counters, budget consumption via atomic `INCRBY` behind a Lua script that checks the cap in the same round trip, and the rule cache. Postgres stays the source of truth.
- Build it as a module inside the backend behind a clean interface, not a microservice on day one. Extracting it now adds a network hop to the order-assignment critical path for no benefit.
- **Fail open.** Any evaluation error returns a zero bonus and lets the order flow. This going down must never stop a pickup being assigned.
- Migrations must be additive — new tables only, no `ALTER` on hot tables, new columns nullable with defaults.

<Callout kind="warning">

**Stack hazard.** `CashkrBackend` and `new-2026-admin-panel` are separate Prisma projects pointed at the same database and `public` schema, sharing one `_prisma_migrations` table — this is what produced the WipeStatus failure. Create all surge tables and enums from one project and mark them introspected-only in the other, or this feature reproduces that exact bug.

</Callout>

**For testers**

- Confirm a rule change creates a new version rather than mutating the old one, and that past quotes still resolve against the version they were calculated under.
- Confirm both caps bind independently — a high-value order should still be limited by the absolute cap, and a low-value order by the percentage cap.
- Drive a city to its daily budget with concurrent offers and confirm the cap is not overshot.
- Confirm the 70% budget alert fires once, not repeatedly.
- Confirm the kill switch stops all bonuses while orders continue to be offered and assigned.
- Confirm dry-run simulation changes nothing in production.
- Confirm a pincode change after order creation resets escalation to zero and triggers a re-quote.
