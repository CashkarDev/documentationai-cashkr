## Purpose and who uses this

Used by: Store Partnerships / Vendor Management / Ops and Support teams.

Use this script to review, approve, and reactivate a store vendor after a temporary Hold, temporary suspension, or inactivity, ensuring all criteria are met and the decision is fully auditable before restoring access.

<Callout kind="alert">

- Do not reactivate vendors who are permanently deactivated or blacklisted.
- Always verify financial and compliance settlement before reactivation.
- Log every decision with evidence in the Admin Panel for audit.

</Callout>

## When reactivation applies vs when it does not

### When reactivation applies

Reactivation applies only to vendors who are temporarily paused and still eligible to work with Cashkr:

- Vendors placed on **Hold** due to SLA issues, operational issues, or pending dues that are now resolved.
- Vendors under **temporary suspension** or technical block where the root cause is fixed.
- Vendors **inactive** for some time, where you have confirmed that they want to resume and still meet current requirements.

Treat these vendors as partners who had a temporary interruption, not a new onboarding, as long as they remain eligible.

### When reactivation does not apply

Reactivation does not apply when the relationship is considered closed or blocked.

<Callout kind="alert">

- **Permanent deactivation**: Treat as closure of relationship. Do not reactivate by default. Any exception requires senior management approval and clear written justification.
- **Fraud, severe misconduct, or unpaid dues after escalation**: Treat as **blacklist**. Do not reactivate or promise any future onboarding unless written exception from management.
- If you are unsure whether a vendor is on Hold, temporarily suspended, or permanently deactivated, pause the call and confirm in the Admin Panel or with Vendor Ops before committing anything.

</Callout>

## Eligibility checklist before approving reactivation

Confirm these points before you request approval to reactivate a store vendor:

- **Original issue resolved**  
  The reason for Hold or suspension is fixed and documented (for example: SLA breach addressed, dues cleared, missing reports submitted).

- **No open disputes or audits**  
  No active fraud flags, refund disputes, or ongoing compliance reviews against the vendor.

- **Wallet and credits ready**  
  Vendor has enough wallet balance or credit limit to handle expected store orders, including payouts and reversals.

- **Credit settlement status clear**  
  Past credit dues and adjustments are settled in the system.

- **Store mapping readiness**  
  Vendor is mapped to appropriate stores or zones, and there is no conflicting mapping in Admin Panel.

- **Vendor readiness confirmed**  
  Vendor confirms they understand current SLAs and is ready to resume orders consistently.

- **Compliance acknowledgement**  
  Vendor verbally acknowledges that repeated issues can lead to immediate Hold or permanent deactivation.

If any of these are not fully met, do not proceed with reactivation. Move to remediation or deny reactivation with clear next steps.

## Reactivation workflow (end-to-end)

Use this workflow for all store vendor reactivation cases.

<Steps>

<Step title="Receive and log reactivation request" icon="help-circle">

- Capture how the vendor requested reactivation (call, app message, WhatsApp, email).
- Confirm vendor identity: name, store name, registered mobile, and city.
- Note the initial reason for Hold/suspension or inactivity, based on Admin Panel and past documentation.

</Step>

<Step title="Confirm current status (Hold, suspension, or deactivation)" icon="settings">

- Open the Admin Panel and check the vendor status: Hold, temporarily suspended, active, or permanently deactivated.
- Review any tags such as fraud, unpaid dues, or compliance flags.
- If the account is shown as permanently deactivated or blacklisted, stop here and follow the "not applicable" rules.

</Step>

<Step title="Verify resolution of original issue" icon="check-circle">

- Re-check the original Hold or suspension reason: SLA, unprofessional behavior, low activity, wallet or credit default, missing reports, disputes, or audit.
- Confirm the resolution with evidence: system records, payment proof, SLA adherence records, or internal notes.
- Update internal comments with a short summary of how the issue was resolved.

</Step>

<Step title="Run financial and readiness checks" icon="bar-chart">

- Verify wallet balance and credit availability in the Admin Panel against typical daily or weekly volume for that store.
- Confirm that there are no pending credit settlements or overdue amounts.
- Check if the vendor app is updated and confirm they will keep location and notifications enabled.

</Step>

<Step title="Validate store mapping and access configuration" icon="folder">

- Review store mapping and area coverage in the Admin Panel to ensure the vendor is assigned to correct stores.
- Check for any overlapping or conflicting mapping with other vendors in the same location.
- Decide whether to resume with existing mapping or adjust to a more suitable set of stores before activation.

</Step>

<Step title="Seek and record approvals" icon="shield">

- For Hold clearance where all issues are resolved, obtain Ops or Vendor Ops approval as per process.
- For more serious histories (repeated Holds, prior suspensions), escalate to Ops Manager or Head before proceeding.
- Document the name, designation, and date of the approver in the case notes.

</Step>

<Step title="Update status in Admin Panel" icon="terminal">

- Once approvals are in place, change the vendor status from Hold/suspended to Active, following the internal process.
- Double-check that the correct vendor account and store mapping are updated.
- Add a clear, concise note: reason for reactivation, date, and your name.

</Step>

<Step title="Communicate decision and required actions" icon="users">

- Inform the vendor of the outcome (approved or not approved) using the templates below.
- Always guide them to log out and log back in to refresh access after reactivation.
- Remind them about key expectations: SLAs, credit settlement, communication, and app usage.

</Step>

<Step title="Monitor performance after reactivation" icon="monitor">

- For the first few days after reactivation, track SLA adherence, order handling quality, and escalation patterns.
- Flag any early warning signs (missed pickups, delayed reporting, payment issues) to Ops for quick intervention.
- Add brief monitoring notes so future reviewers see how the vendor performed after coming back.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout kind="alert">

- Do not promise exact timelines for when orders or leads will start again after reactivation. Explain that order flow depends on area, store traffic, and credits.
- Always instruct the vendor to log out and log back in after reactivation to refresh their access and mapping.
- Ensure every status change has supporting notes and references so an auditor can understand what happened and why.

</Callout>

## Communication templates (written)

Use these templates for email, WhatsApp, or in-app messages. Adjust placeholders and details as needed.

### Reactivation approved – confirmation message

Subject: Your Cashkr Store Vendor Account Has Been Reactivated

Hi [Vendor Name],

We have completed the review of your Cashkr store vendor account. Your previous issues have been resolved and your account is now **reactivated**.

You can now log in to the Cashkr Store App and continue handling store orders as they are assigned to you.

Before you start:

- Log out and log back in once to refresh your access and store mapping.
- Ensure your wallet balance or credits are sufficient for upcoming orders.
- Follow all Cashkr SLAs, reporting timelines, and communication guidelines.

Consistent performance and compliance will help keep your account active without interruption.

Regards,  
Cashkr Vendor Management Team

### Reactivation not approved – missing requirements

Subject: Update on Your Cashkr Store Vendor Reactivation Request

Hi [Vendor Name],

We have reviewed your request to reactivate your Cashkr store vendor account.

Right now, we are **not able to approve reactivation** because of the following reason(s):

- [Example: Pending credit or wallet dues]
- [Example: Ongoing dispute or audit review]
- [Example: Repeated SLA or behavior issues under review]

Next steps:

- Resolve the above point(s) and keep proof or confirmation ready.
- Once done, contact us again so we can re-check your account status.

We will review your profile again after the pending requirements are completed and update you on the final decision.

Regards,  
Cashkr Vendor Management Team

<Callout kind="alert">

- Do not commit that reactivation will be approved after the vendor completes the pending items. Say that the account will be **re-reviewed**.
- Do not promise restoration of old leads or specific order volumes. Clarify that orders depend on area, demand, and credits.

</Callout>

## What to verify in Admin Panel

Before you reactivate, cross-check the following in the Admin Panel:

- **Vendor status and history**  
  Confirm if the vendor is on Hold, temporarily suspended, or permanently deactivated. Review past Holds, SLA issues, and any blacklisting notes.

- **Financials**  
  Check wallet balance, credit limit, recent settlements, and any overdue or reversed amounts.

- **Disputes, audits, and flags**  
  Review open disputes, fraud investigations, or audit remarks. Ensure there are no unresolved high-risk flags.

- **Store mapping and permissions**  
  Confirm assigned stores, service areas, and access levels. Make sure the mapping matches current business rules.

- **Internal notes and approvals**  
  Read earlier comments to understand context. Record new approval details (who approved, when, and why).

## Post-reactivation monitoring checklist

After you reactivate a store vendor, monitor their performance closely for an initial observation period.

Focus on:

- **Order handling quality**  
  Vendor accepts, processes, and closes orders as per defined SLAs, without unnecessary delays.

- **Customer and store feedback**  
  Watch for complaints about behavior, communication, or payment handling from store staff or customers.

- **Reporting and documentation**  
  Daily reports, reconciliations, and required uploads are completed on time.

- **Financial discipline**  
  Credits and wallet usage remain healthy, with no repeated payment delays or unexplained shortfalls.

- **Responsiveness**  
  Vendor responds to support calls, messages, and escalations within a reasonable time.

If you see repeated or serious issues, coordinate with Ops or Vendor Ops to consider putting the vendor back on Hold or, in high-risk cases, recommending deactivation as per policy.

## Edge cases and troubleshooting

Use these patterns when the situation is not straightforward.

<ExpandableGroup>

<Expandable title="Vendor says account is reactivated but app shows no access" default-open="false">

- First, re-check status in Admin Panel to confirm the account is actually Active.
- Ask the vendor to log out of the app and log back in to refresh permissions.
- Confirm if the app is updated to the latest version and if device settings allow location and notifications.
- If status is Active but access is still blocked, log an Admin Panel or tech ticket with screenshots and time of issue.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Dues or settlements not fully cleared">

- Check the exact pending amount or unsettled item in the Admin Panel.
- Explain clearly that reactivation can only be considered after financials are regularized.
- Guide the vendor on how to clear dues or share proof of payment with Accounts or Vendor Ops.
- Add a note that reactivation review will be re-opened only after settlement is visible or confirmed in the system.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Open dispute or ongoing audit" default-open="false">

- Do not proceed with reactivation while a dispute, fraud check, or audit is open.
- Inform the vendor that the account is under internal review and that the decision will be taken after the review closes.
- Coordinate with the Compliance or Audit owner to understand expected next steps.
- Update internal notes to show you paused reactivation due to open review.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Store mapping changed or unclear" default-open="false">

- If the vendor reports they cannot see expected stores after reactivation, verify current mapping in Admin Panel.
- Confirm whether business rules have changed (for example, different vendors assigned to that store or area).
- Adjust mapping only after Ops or Vendor Ops approval and document why the mapping was changed.
- Communicate clearly to the vendor which stores they are now mapped to and manage expectations on order volume.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Repeated issues after past reactivations" default-open="false">

- Review the vendor history for multiple Holds, SLA breaches, or behavior problems after earlier reactivations.
- Escalate such cases to Ops Manager or Head for a decision; do not reactivate at your own level.
- Consider whether the pattern indicates that a long-term or permanent deactivation is more appropriate.
- Record the rationale and final decision in detail for future reference.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Vendor inactive for a long time and wants to return" default-open="false">

- Treat long-term inactivity without Hold as a light re-onboarding.
- Verify KYC details and documents if they are old or if policy has changed since the last active period.
- Confirm that the vendor still has staff, infrastructure, and interest to handle store walk-ins regularly.
- Provide a short refresher on store app usage, pricing logic, and key SLAs.
- Restart with a manageable set of stores and monitor performance closely before expanding the mapping.

</Expandable>

</ExpandableGroup>

## Key reminders for Vendor Management team

- Reactivation rewards compliance and readiness after a Hold, not entitlement to automatic access.
- Never override deactivation or blacklist decisions without senior approval and strong documentation.
- Check status, evidence, and approvals in Admin Panel before changing vendor access.
- Use calm, professional language and always close the conversation with clear next steps for the vendor.
