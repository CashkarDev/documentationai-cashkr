## Who this SOP is for and why it exists

<Callout kind="info">

**Audience:** Sales, CX, Vendor Ops, and Marketing teams who speak to customers or vendors about device pricing.

**Purpose:** Use a single, consistent logic to explain:

- When price can change before or after pickup
- Why those changes happen
- How the Cashkr pricing engine decides value
- How to communicate clearly and avoid confusion or disputes

**Goals:**

- Reduce customer confusion
- Improve trust and transparency
- Standardize communication across teams
- Reduce disputes and escalations
- Increase conversion from quote to payment

</Callout>

## The three stages of price

Customers see and experience the price in three clear stages. Each stage has its own logic and valid reasons for change.

<Tabs>

<Tab title="Stage 1 – Estimated Price (Before pickup)">

The estimated price appears on the website or app based on customer inputs **before pickup**.

**Inputs that decide the estimated price:**

- Device model
- Variant (storage, RAM, processor)
- Selected condition
- Selected known issues
- Charger availability
- Current market pricing for that model

**When can the estimated price change?**

- **Customer changes condition or issues**
  - Changes from "Excellent" to "Good"
  - Adds issues like "Screen cracked"
  - Selects "No charger"
  - → Price drops or adjusts immediately in the app.

- **Customer changes model or variant**
  - 128 GB → 64 GB → price reduces
  - i5 → i7 → price increases
  - M1 → i3 → price changes significantly
  - → System recalculates based on actual variant.

- **Market price update (daily/weekly)**
  - Pricing team updates base prices due to:
    - Resale demand
    - Spare parts cost
    - New model launches
    - General market drop or increase
  - → Affects only **future** estimated prices shown before pickup.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Stage 2 – Adjusted Price (After vendor inspection)">

The adjusted price appears **after the vendor physically inspects the device**. This is where the system corrects any mismatch between customer inputs and the real device condition.

**What can change the price at this stage?**

- **A. Device condition differences (wrong inputs earlier)**
  - Customer selected "No scratches" → vendor finds a cracked screen → deduction applies.
  - Customer selected "Battery good" → vendor finds battery health under 80% → deduction applies.
  - Customer selected "All OK" → vendor finds camera not working → deduction applies.

- **B. Hidden or unexpected issues found during inspection**
  - Green line on display
  - Camera blur
  - Dead pixels
  - Touch issues
  - Face ID or Touch ID failure
  - Bent or twisted body
  - Heating issues
  - Laptop hinges loose
  - Laptop battery backup under 1 hour
  - → These are not visible to the customer without detailed checks, so they are corrected at inspection time.

- **C. Device age and battery cycles (laptop / Mac)**
  - Older devices get lower value.
  - Battery with:
    - Very high cycle count
    - Poor backup
    - "Service Recommended" warning
  - → Pricing engine applies a reduction for necessary battery replacement.

- **D. Accessories missing or not original**
  - No charger
  - No cable
  - Fake or incompatible charger
  - Very old or corroded charger
  - → Deduction applies because resale value drops.

- **E. Water damage or physical damage**
  - Any sign of liquid contact
  - Internal damage or major body damage
  - → Leads to a reduced price for repair cost and risk.

</Tab>

<Tab title="Stage 3 – Final Price (Agreed value)">

The final price is the value the customer **accepts and confirms by OTP** after seeing the adjusted price.

**Final price becomes locked when:**

- Vendor completes inspection
- System generates the updated valuation
- Customer agrees to the new price
- Customer shares OTP, which triggers:
  - Payment
  - Pickup confirmation
  - Order completion

If the customer **rejects** the updated price:

- No charge applies
- Order is closed without pickup

</Tab>

</Tabs>

## Valid reasons for price change

Each stage allows specific, valid reasons for price change. Use this list as your reference when explaining to customers.

### 1. Customer provided wrong or incomplete inputs

- Selected a better condition than actual (for example, "All good" but there are scratches or dents)
- Missed issues like screen lines, camera problems, or touch issues
- Entered battery as "good" but actual battery health is low

Result: Pricing engine recalculates after inspection and applies deductions.

### 2. Vendor finds real condition issues

- Physical inspection always reveals the true condition.
- Any discovered defect that was **not** reflected in the original inputs can reduce the price.

Result: System updates price based on verified device condition.

### 3. Market value drops or changes

- New model launches
- Change in resale demand
- Change in spare parts cost
- General market drop or increase for that category

Result: Base pricing in the engine changes, affecting estimated and later valuations.

### 4. Wrong variant or model selected

- Customer chooses the wrong **variant**:
  - Selected 256 GB but actual is 128 GB
  - Selected i7 but actual is i5
- Customer chooses the wrong **model**:
  - Very common when customers are unsure or confused

Result: System corrects the price to match the real model and variant.

### 5. Accessories missing or fake

- No charger or cable
- Fake or non-original charger
- Very old or damaged charger

Result: Deduction applies because resale value reduces.

### 6. Hidden or technical issues (especially for laptops/Macs)

- Low battery backup (for example, only 30 minutes)
- High battery cycles
- Fan noise, heating, or performance issues
- Display flicker or dead pixels
- Other internal hardware issues

Result: System applies deductions for repair or replacement cost.

## When price does not change

Price does **not** change for the following reasons. Use this list to confidently reassure customers.

- Customer changes address
- Customer delays pickup or reschedules
- Festival sales or promotions
- Referral code usage (referral adds bonus, it does not change base device price)
- Vendor personal preference (vendor cannot edit price manually)

## Non‑negotiable rules about price and vendor control

<Callout kind="alert">

**Rule 1 – Vendor cannot edit price manually**

- Vendor cannot type or manually change any price.
- Vendor only selects condition options in the app.
- The Cashkr pricing engine calculates and recalculates the price.
- This ensures full transparency and fairness to customers and vendors.

**Customer-facing line to use:**

"The vendor does not decide the price; the Cashkr app does."

</Callout>

<Callout kind="alert">

**Rule 2 – Sales and CX must not promise fixed price lock**

- Do not guarantee that the online estimated price will remain the same after inspection.
- Always explain that final price depends on actual device condition at pickup.
- Never promise that price will increase later.
- Do not blame the vendor for any change; explain that the system updated the price.

</Callout>

## Customer communication scripts (how to explain price changes)

Use these ready scripts to answer the most common questions and objections.

<ExpandableGroup>

<Expandable title="Customer asks: Why did the price change?">

**Key logic to explain:**

- Actual device condition at pickup may differ from the condition selected earlier.
- The app updates the price automatically when the vendor enters the real condition and issues.

**Suggested script:**

> "The price changed because the actual condition of the device is different from what was selected earlier. The app automatically updates it based on the inspection details."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Customer accuses vendor of cheating">

**Key logic to explain:**

- Vendor has no manual control over pricing.
- All changes come from the Cashkr system based on inputs.

**Suggested script:**

> "The vendor cannot change the price manually. The Cashkr system adjusts the price based on the actual issues and condition found during inspection."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Customer mentions competitor price">

**Key logic to explain:**

- Cashkr is open to matching competitor quotes where possible.
- You need a proof of the final competitor quote.

**Suggested script:**

> "We match competitor quotes whenever possible. Please share their final quote screenshot, and we will try our best to match it."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Customer questions market drop">

**Key logic to explain:**

- Prices depend on current market demand and resale value.
- When market changes, all similar devices are affected.

**Suggested script:**

> "Prices depend on current market demand. If the market drops this week, the resale values of all similar models also drop, so the price may be lower than before."

</Expandable>

</ExpandableGroup>

## Common scenarios to train teams

Use these scenarios in role-plays and training so agents and vendors practice explaining price changes confidently.

<ExpandableGroup>

<Expandable title="Scenario 1: Customer selected 'All good' but screen has scratch">

- Customer chose "All good" or similar high condition.
- Vendor finds visible screen scratch during inspection.
- System applies a deduction for screen damage.

**How to explain:**

> "The online quote was based on 'All good' condition, but the screen has a visible scratch. The system has applied a standard deduction for this issue."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Scenario 2: Battery health shows 74%">

- Customer marked battery as "good".
- Vendor checks and sees battery health at 74%.
- Engine applies a deduction for required battery replacement.

**How to explain:**

> "The battery health is at 74%, which means it needs replacement sooner. The app automatically reduces the price to adjust for this battery cost."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Scenario 3: Laptop backup only 30 minutes">

- Customer did not know exact backup time.
- Vendor tests and finds backup is only around 30 minutes.
- System applies a large deduction for laptop battery change.

**How to explain:**

> "Your laptop battery backup is only around 30 minutes, so it needs a new battery. The system has reduced the price to cover this replacement."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Scenario 4: Customer has no charger">

- Customer selected or confirms "No charger" at pickup.
- Resale value drops because device is incomplete.
- Pricing engine deducts for missing charger.

**How to explain:**

> "The quote with charger and without charger is different. Because the device is without a charger, the app has applied a deduction to match the actual resale value."

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="Scenario 5: Customer selected wrong variant">

- Customer selected a higher variant (for example, 256 GB or i7).
- Actual device is a lower variant (for example, 128 GB or i5).
- Price is corrected down to the real variant value.

**How to explain:**

> "The online quote was generated for a higher variant, but your device is the lower variant. The price has been corrected to match the actual model and configuration."

</Expandable>

</ExpandableGroup>

## Rules for Sales and CX teams during calls

These rules keep communication consistent and aligned with the pricing engine.

- **Always explain that price depends on actual condition.**
  - Mention that the online quote is based on self-declared condition.
  - Reinforce that inspection confirms the final value.

- **Do not promise price lock.**
  - Avoid saying "price will not change" before inspection.
  - Always say "final price depends on actual device condition."

- **Never blame the vendor.**
  - Explain that the system, not the vendor, updates the price.
  - Use the line: "The Cashkr app decides the price based on condition."

- **Never promise a price increase.**
  - Do not suggest that inspection will automatically increase price.
  - Focus on accurate, fair valuation instead of "better price later."

- **Encourage customers to select the correct condition on the website/app.**
  - Guide them to be honest about scratches, dents, battery health, and issues.
  - Explain that accurate inputs reduce surprises at pickup.

- **Always use the standard script.**
  - Follow the customer communication scripts in this SOP.
  - Escalate only when there is a genuine issue, not when logic is correct.

## One‑page summary (quick reference)

Use this as a mental checklist during calls or training.

### Price changes because:

- Real condition is found at inspection.
- Customer selected wrong or incomplete condition earlier.
- Market prices for that model change.
- Accessories are missing, fake, or damaged.
- Hidden or internal issues are detected (battery, display, heating, hinges, etc.).
- Wrong model or variant was selected initially.

### Price does not change because:

- Vendor personal decision or negotiation.
- Sales or CX intervention or promise.
- Random, unexplained change in the system.
- Customer location, address change, or pickup delay.
- Festival offers or referral codes (these only affect bonuses, not base price).
