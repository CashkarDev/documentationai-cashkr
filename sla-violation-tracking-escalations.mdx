## Purpose and ownership

Use this SOP to track SLA violations and apply consistent actions across Operations, Vendor Ops, CX, BD, and Management.

SLA violation tracking aims to:

- Ensure timely pickups and order completion
- Maintain vendor accountability
- Reduce customer complaints
- Improve and stabilize order completion rate
- Provide a clear, shared escalation pathway across teams

## Quick checklist for handling an SLA issue

Use this as your fast path from detection to action:

- **Detect issue** from Admin Panel, customer complaint, CX follow-up, or vendor activity logs.
- **Identify SLA type** (delay, wrong failure, payment issue, no contact, behaviour, fraud).
- **CX logs violation** clearly in the Admin Panel with notes and context.
- **Ops validates** using order timeline, vendor logs, and past violation history.
- **Vendor Ops contacts vendor** (except direct fraud cases) to understand reason and clarify expectations.
- **Update SLA tracker** with vendor name, order ID, violation type, reason, and action taken.
- **Check weekly threshold** and apply the mapped action (warning, coaching, penalty, hold, or deactivation in severe cases).
- **Inform CX** of final decision so they can update the customer if needed.
- **BD and Management** review patterns weekly via reports and dashboards.

## What SLA means at Cashkr

Service Level Agreement (SLA) defines the minimum operational standards vendors must follow during pickups. Any failure to meet these is an SLA violation.

Core SLAs for vendors:

- Call the customer within 15–20 minutes of assignment.
- Reach the pickup location within the scheduled time.
- Complete the order without unnecessary delay.
- Communicate politely and professionally at all times.
- Follow device inspection rules accurately.
- Avoid fraud or falsifying conditions.
- Complete payment instantly after OTP confirmation.

## SLA violation categories

There are six primary SLA violation categories. Always tag and track violations using these.

### 1. Delayed pickup

Vendor does not reach on time or delays starting the assignment.

Examples:

- Assigned but no call made to the customer for more than 20 minutes
- Vendor promises an ETA but does not follow it
- Vendor takes too long to start the trip after confirmation

### 2. Improper failure or wrong failure reason

Vendor marks the order as failed with an incorrect or unfair reason.

Examples:

- Customer is available but vendor marks status as `Not Available`
- Vendor does not check the device properly before failing
- Vendor fails the order to avoid taking low-price devices

### 3. Missed, incorrect, or delayed payment

Vendor does not complete payment correctly or on time.

Examples:

- Payment delay after successful pickup and OTP
- Vendor leaves the location without completing payment
- Vendor sends payment to the wrong UPI due to carelessness

### 4. Vendor not contacting customer

Vendor is assigned but does not contact the customer.

Examples:

- No call placed after assignment within SLA time
- Customer reports no communication from vendor
- Only missed calls or no genuine attempt to coordinate

### 5. Behaviour or professionalism issues

Vendor behaves in a way that is rude, unprofessional, or against Cashkr standards.

Examples:

- Using abusive or aggressive tone with the customer
- Forcing the customer to accept a lower price
- Not being presentable or not wearing ID when required

### 6. Fraud or intentional manipulation

Vendor attempts to cheat, misrepresent, or misuse the system.

<Callout kind="alert">

Treat all fraud or intentional manipulation cases as **critical**. Escalate to Vendor Ops and Management immediately. Deactivation is expected in severe or confirmed cases.

</Callout>

Examples:

- Entering a false device condition to reduce price intentionally
- Trying to reduce price by misrepresenting issues
- Misusing OTP or attempting to complete the order without customer consent
- Sharing fake or edited payment screenshots

## How SLA violations are tracked

Ops, CX, and Vendor Ops track SLA violations through:

- Admin Panel order timeline
- Vendor activity logs
- Customer complaints
- CX follow-up calls
- Vendor Ops reports
- BD team city-level performance analysis

In the Admin Panel, use:

- **Orders → SLA Violations List** to see:
  - Vendor name
  - Order ID
  - Violation type
  - City
  - Timestamp
  - Number of violations for that vendor in the current week

## Weekly SLA violation thresholds and actions

Use weekly violation count per vendor to decide the level of action.

| Weekly violation count | Action                           |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1–2                    | Warning message                  |
| 3–4                    | Vendor Ops call and coaching     |
| 5                      | Vendor review and potential penalty |
| 6+                     | Vendor hold until further notice |

## SLA standards for vendor performance

Use these time standards to decide whether an SLA has been breached.

| Activity                        | SLA time                          |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Call customer after assignment | Within 15 minutes                 |
| Start trip after confirmation  | Within 20 minutes                 |
| Reach location                 | 0–60 minutes (as per city)        |
| Inspection completion          | 5–10 minutes                      |
| Payment completion             | Immediately after OTP confirmation |

Any deviation from these standards is a potential SLA violation and should be logged and reviewed.

## Escalation flow

Use this standard flow from first detection to final action.

<Steps>
  <Step title="CX identifies issue" icon="alert-triangle">

  - CX identifies a slow or problematic vendor from a customer complaint, call, or Admin Panel.
  - CX logs the SLA violation in the Admin Panel with a clear note, such as:
    - `Vendor not calling customer`
    - `Customer waiting 45 minutes`

  </Step>

  <Step title="Ops validates" icon="settings">

  - Ops reviews the order timeline and vendor activity logs.
  - Ops checks previous violations for the same vendor.
  - If confirmed, Ops marks the issue as a valid SLA violation and informs Vendor Ops.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Vendor Ops contacts vendor" icon="phone">

  - Vendor Ops calls the vendor (except in direct fraud cases where immediate escalation may apply).
  - Understand the vendor's reason and clarify expectations and SLAs.
  - Warn the vendor where needed and record the explanation.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Update SLA tracker" icon="edit">

  - Add or update an entry in the SLA tracker with:
    - Vendor name
    - Order ID
    - Violation type
    - Reason given by vendor
    - Action taken or planned

  </Step>

  <Step title="Apply appropriate action" icon="check-circle">

  - Check the vendor's weekly violation count.
  - Apply the mapped action:
    - **Warning (soft action)** for minor delays or first violations.
    - **Coaching call** where Vendor Ops explains why the violation occurred and how to improve.
    - **Penalty or credit deduction** for vendors who repeatedly ignore SLAs, including:
      - Credit deduction (for example, 5–20 credits)
      - Restricting order unlock limit
    - **Hold vendor** when:
      - Weekly violations exceed 5
      - There are repeated delays or payment issues
      - There are repeated customer complaints
    - **Deactivation** in severe cases such as:
      - Fraud or OTP misuse
      - Payment misconduct
      - Harassment or serious behaviour issues
      - Repetitive violations despite previous actions

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Team responsibilities

### CX: customer experience protocol for SLA-delayed orders

When an order is delayed or has an SLA issue, CX should:

- Apologize to the customer for the delay or inconvenience.
- Call the vendor immediately to check status.
- Share an updated and realistic ETA with the customer.
- Log the issue clearly in the Admin Panel.
- Escalate to Ops if the vendor is unresponsive or repeatedly delaying.
- Trigger reassignment when necessary to protect customer experience.

### BD: responsibilities in SLA violations

BD (Business Development and Area Management) focuses on preventing SLA issues by improving coverage and supply, not by disciplining vendors.

BD responsibilities:

- Ensure adequate vendor coverage per pincode.
- Identify areas with repeated SLA failures or poor coverage.
- Highlight operational bottlenecks causing delays or failures.
- Coordinate with Vendor Ops to bring new vendors into weak areas.
- Improve vendor distribution in high-order or high-complaint zones.

### Management: reporting and dashboards

Management reviews SLA performance to make scaling and pruning decisions.

Key reports and dashboards:

- Weekly SLA violation report
- City-level SLA heat map
- Vendor-level SLA performance and rankings
- List of repeat offenders
- SLA performance vs order completion rate

Use these insights to adjust vendor base, city strategies, and training focus.

## One-page quick summary

Use this as your training and reference snapshot.

**SLA violations include:**

- Delayed pickup or no customer call
- Wrong failure or misreported reason
- Miscommunication or wrong inspection
- Payment issues (missed, delayed, or wrong UPI)
- Vendor behaviour or professionalism issues
- Fraud or intentional manipulation

**Weekly thresholds and actions:**

- 1–2 violations: warning
- 3–4 violations: Vendor Ops call and coaching
- 5 violations: vendor review and potential penalty
- 6+ violations: vendor hold until further notice

**Escalation chain:**

- CX → Identify issue and log violation
- Ops → Validate using logs and history
- Vendor Ops → Contact vendor and take action
- BD → Improve area and vendor availability
- Management → Review reports and approve major actions

**Action ladder:**

- Warning → Coaching → Penalty → Hold → Deactivate (for severe or repeated cases)
