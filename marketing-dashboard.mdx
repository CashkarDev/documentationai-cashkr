## Overview

The marketing dashboard summarizes your Google Ads performance, website traffic, and orders in one place so you can see how ad spend turns into paying customers.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with the Google Ads scorecards" icon="bar-chart" title-type="p">
    Look at high-level performance: spend, clicks, conversions, CPC, impressions, and CTR. This tells you if performance is directionally on track before you dive into details.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Drill into campaigns, keywords, and ad groups" icon="search" title-type="p">
    Use the clicks by search keyword, conversions by campaign, top campaigns table, and top ad group by clicks to see which themes and campaigns drive volume and results.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect spend to orders over time" icon="timeline" title-type="p">
    Review spend by time, cost and orders received over time, conversion by date, and CPA and conversions by day to understand trends and whether extra spend is driving more orders.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Validate orders and channels" icon="check-circle" title-type="p">
    Compare orders received by Cashkr vs Analytics and the session breakdown by channel to confirm which sources drive qualified traffic and completed orders.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Monitor SEO alongside paid ads" icon="database" title-type="p">
    Use the query ranking by Google Search Console section to see which organic queries bring traffic, and compare them against your paid search keywords.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keyword and campaign performance

These widgets help you see which search themes and campaigns actually drive traffic and conversions.

### Clicks by search keyword

A bar chart shows which search terms (for example, `sell old phone`, `sell my phone`) are driving the most clicks. This helps you see what people are actually searching for.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Look for high-click keywords that also appear in your top converting campaigns. Those are strong candidates for higher bids and more ad budget.
</Callout>

### Conversions by campaign

This chart shows which ad campaigns are converting (turning clicks into orders). `Previous Locations - Phone` appears to be the top performer.

Use this to identify campaigns you should scale and those you may want to pause or optimize.

### Top 5 campaigns table

A ranked table lists your best campaigns with:

- Conversions

- Clicks

- Impressions

- CTR

- Avg CPC

- Cost

This quickly tells you where your money is going and what is working.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Compare CPC, CTR, and conversions together. A slightly higher CPC is acceptable when the campaign delivers many conversions at a good CPA.
</Callout>

### Top ad group by clicks

A table ranks your ad groups (for example, `sell old mobile`, `vivo api`, `phone`) by clicks. This shows which ad groups attract the most traffic.

Use this to refine ad copy and keyword sets inside high-traffic ad groups.

## Spend and orders over time

These charts help you understand how daily spend relates to orders and conversions.

### Spend by time

A line or area chart shows how your daily ad spend fluctuates over time.

Use this to spot days when spend spiked or dropped and correlate those changes with orders and conversions.

### Cost and orders received over time

A combo chart (bars plus line) compares:

- Daily ad spend (bars)

- Orders received (line)

The goal is to see if spending more actually brings more orders.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  If spend increases but the orders line stays flat or drops, investigate campaigns, keywords, and landing pages for that period.
</Callout>

### Conversion by date

A line chart tracks daily conversions over time. This helps you spot trends:

- Are conversions going up, down, or flat?

- Do changes in spend or new campaigns impact conversion counts?

### CPA and conversions by day

A combo chart shows:

- **CPA** (Cost Per Acquisition) as orange bars

- **Conversions** as a blue line

Ideally, CPA goes down while conversions go up.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Use this chart to judge efficiency. A lower CPA with stable or rising conversions indicates better performance, even if total spend changes.
</Callout>

## Orders and tracking sources

These sections help you understand how orders are recorded across systems and which traffic channels perform best.

### Orders received (two pie charts)

Two pie charts show order sources from different tracking systems:

- **By Cashkr Orders**: Breakdown of order sources from your internal system.

- **By Analytics**: Same data but as tracked by Google Analytics (helps you cross-check).

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Differences between Cashkr Orders and Analytics can come from tracking issues, ad blockers, or users switching devices. Use both views together to validate trends rather than relying on exact matches.
</Callout>

### Session breakdown by channel

A table shows where your website traffic comes from, such as:

- **Organic** (Google search)

- **Paid Search** (Google Ads)

- **Direct** (typed URL)

- Other tracked channels

Each row shows event counts like sessions, orders started, and orders completed. This tells you which channels are actually bringing paying customers.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Prioritize channels that drive high completed orders, not just high sessions. A channel with fewer visits but higher completion rate can be more valuable.
</Callout>

## SEO performance (Google Search Console)

Query Ranking by GSC (Google Search Console) is represented as a bar chart showing which organic search queries (for example, `cashkr`, `sell old phone`, `sell phone online`) are getting the most clicks from Google Search. This is your SEO performance, not paid ads.

Use this to:

- See which terms you already rank for organically.

- Align SEO content and paid keyword strategy.

- Identify organic queries that might deserve dedicated landing pages or campaigns.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  When the same queries perform well in both paid and organic search, consider building stronger landing pages and content around them. That can improve both SEO and paid conversion rates over time.
</Callout>
