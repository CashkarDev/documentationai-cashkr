## Role & reporting line

- **Role title:** Vendor SLA & Quality Associate (SLA Associate) – Cashkr  
- **Department:** Operations  
- **Reports to (primary):** Operations Manager  

**Works closely with:**

- **Head of Operations / Operations & Pricing Manager:** For serious vendor actions and policies.
- **Area & Serviceability Manager:** For vendor holds/closures and area-serviceability impact.
- **CX / Customer Support Team:** For complaints, failures, and escalations from customer side.
- **In-house pickup associates:** For behaviour and SLA checks.
- **External vendor partners:** For SLA clarifications and warnings.
- **Data Analyst:** For SLA dashboards, failure trends, and vendor performance data.

## Role purpose

Ensure that every failed or problematic order has a clear, accurate reason and that:

- Vendors are not blamed for issues caused by customers or system gaps.
- Genuine vendor-side issues are detected, documented and acted on.
- Vendor SLA and quality standards are maintained and improved over time through data, audits and actions.

**In short: protect fairness for both customer and company by separating customer-side failures from vendor-side failures, and cleaning up bad vendor performance.**

## Key result areas (KRAs)

- **KRA 1 – SLA & failure reason validation:** Review failed, cancelled and problem orders and make sure the root cause is correctly classified (customer-side vs vendor-side vs system/operations).
- **KRA 2 – Vendor quality & behaviour monitoring:** Continuously monitor vendor performance (SLA adherence, behaviour, call response, visit quality, dispositions) and identify vendors who are not meeting standards.
- **KRA 3 – Vendor action & recommendation:** Based on data and case reviews, recommend actions (warning, training, limited access, hold, closure) to the Operations Manager and Area & Serviceability Manager.
- **KRA 4 – SLA / quality reporting to Operations:** Prepare clear SLA and quality reports for the Operations Manager and Head of Operations so they know why orders are failing, which vendors/areas are problematic, and what actions were taken or are recommended.
- **KRA 5 – Feedback loop for process & training:** Share patterns and insights with Operations, CX and Training so that SOPs, scripts and vendor training can be improved to reduce repeat issues.

## KPIs

### No. 1 – Failure reason classification accuracy

**Description**

How accurately failed or cancelled orders are tagged with the correct root cause (customer vs vendor vs others).

**Metrics**

- Percentage of failed orders with a clear, specific failure reason (no "other/unknown/general").
- Number/percentage of disputes where vendor challenges the reason and is proven correct (low is good).

**Target**

High clarity (near 100% of failed orders with a proper reason) and very low misclassification.

**Data source**

Admin Panel (failure reasons), audit samples, dispute logs.

**Review frequency**

Weekly / Monthly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 1.

### No. 2 – Vendor-side failure rate (monitored & acted on)

**Description**

Measures what fraction of failures are actually vendor-side, and whether those vendors are being tracked and acted upon.

**Formula / Metrics**

- Vendor-side failures ÷ Total failures × 100.
- Number of repeat vendor-side failures by the same vendor without action.

**Target**

Not to force all failures into "customer side", but to ensure vendor-side failures are correctly identified and actioned, and repeat patterns go down over time.

**Data source**

Failure reason tags, vendor IDs, SLA logs.

**Review frequency**

Monthly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 1, KRA 2, KRA 3.

### No. 3 – SLA violation tracking & resolution

**Description**

Tracks how well SLA violations (late pickup, no response, improper failure, wrong payout, etc.) are logged and resolved.

**Metrics**

- Number of SLA violations logged per vendor per week.
- Percentage of SLA violations that have a recorded action (warning, call, training, hold).
- Average time from SLA violation logged to first action taken.

**Target**

All significant SLA violations should have an action; resolution time should reduce over time.

**Data source**

SLA violation log, Admin Panel events, CX/ops escalation tools.

**Review frequency**

Weekly / Monthly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 2, KRA 3, KRA 4.

### No. 4 – Vendor holds / closures based on data

**Description**

Ensures that vendor holds and closures are based on data, not guesswork, and are followed through.

**Metrics**

- Number of vendors put on temporary hold or permanently closed in a month (with clear documented reasons).
- Percentage of holds and closures with supporting SLA and quality evidence.

**Target**

Every strong action (hold/close) must be traceable back to SLA and quality evidence.

**Data source**

Vendor status logs, SLA reports, escalation notes.

**Review frequency**

Monthly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 3, KRA 4.

### No. 5 – Repeat issue reduction

**Description**

Measures whether the same type of problems decrease once highlighted.

**Metrics / Examples**

- Reduction in "vendor no-show" failures in a city/vendor after action.
- Reduction in "improper failure" or "wrong disposition" from specific vendors.

**Target**

Downward trend in repeat issues from vendors after warnings, training, or holds.

**Data source**

Failure reason breakdown by vendor/area, before vs after action.

**Review frequency**

Monthly / Quarterly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 2, KRA 5.

### No. 6 – SLA / quality reporting timeliness

**Description**

How consistently the SLA Associate sends weekly and monthly reports to Operations.

**Formula**

SLA Report On-Time % = Reports sent on or before agreed date ÷ Total scheduled reports × 100.

**Target**

Close to 100%.

**Data source**

Email/Slack timestamps, reporting calendar.

**Review frequency**

Monthly.

**Linked KRAs**

KRA 4.

## Core processes / SOPs owned

The Vendor SLA & Quality Associate (SLA Associate) is owner or co-owner for these SOPs:

- **Failed order & SLA review SOP**
  - **Scope:**
    - Daily and weekly review of:
      - Failed orders.
      - Cancelled orders.
      - "To be failed" or disputed orders.
  - **Classification rules:**
    - Customer-side issues:
      - Not available.
      - Changed mind.
      - Wrong info.
      - No documents.
      - Other genuine customer-side reasons.
    - Vendor-side issues:
      - No-show.
      - Late without reason.
      - Misbehaviour.
      - Improper failure.
      - Not calling.
      - Wrong payout.
      - Other vendor-side performance or behaviour issues.
    - System/operations issues:
      - Wrong assignment.
      - Tech failure.
  - **Documentation:**
    - Documentation of final reason in the system.

- **Vendor quality & behaviour monitoring SOP**
  - **Data points to monitor per vendor/associate:**
    - SLA violations (late pickup, no response, missed payout, etc.).
    - Complaints from CX.
    - Disposition patterns (for example, always failing high-value orders).
  - **Handling guidelines:**
    - Distinguish first-time vs repeated violations.
    - Separate behaviour complaints vs performance issues and act accordingly.

- **Vendor action workflow SOP (with Operations & Area/Serviceability)**
  - **Escalation ladder for vendors:**
    - Soft warning (verbal or call).
    - Written warning.
    - Training or coaching request.
    - Temporary hold or area removal.
    - Permanent closure.
  - **How to work with managers:**
    - Propose actions to the Operations Manager and Area & Serviceability Manager.
    - Record action decisions against each vendor in the system.

- **CX coordination & case clarification SOP**
  - **Coordination with CX:**
    - Work with CX to validate both sides when:
      - A customer blames vendor.
      - A vendor blames customer.
  - **Conflict resolution:**
    - Resolve conflicts by checking:
      - Call logs.
      - Timestamps.
      - Statuses.
  - **Fairness rules:**
    - Ensure vendor is not unfairly blamed for genuine customer-side failures.
    - Ensure customer issues are still taken seriously and escalated where needed.

- **SLA & quality reporting SOP**
  - **Weekly and monthly report structure:**
    - Failures and reasons (customer vs vendor vs system).
    - Top problematic vendors and areas.
    - Actions taken (warnings, holds, closures).
    - Patterns that need systemic fixes (for example, a common issue in a process).
  - **Report sharing:**
    - How to format and share reports with:
      - Operations Manager.
      - Head of Operations.
      - Area & Serviceability.
      - CX and others (if required).

- **Feedback → training / SOP improvement SOP**
  - **Using insights from SLA reviews:**
    - Feed into vendor training modules.
    - Update scripts for vendors.
    - Inform changes in operations or assignment SOPs.
  - **Logging for process change:**
    - Log recurring issues that require process change instead of just vendor punishment.

## Weekly & monthly reporting

### Weekly – "Vendor SLA & Quality Weekly Snapshot"

- **To:**
  - Operations Manager.
  - Head of Operations / Operations & Pricing Manager.
  - Area & Serviceability Manager.
  - CX Lead / CX Team.
  - Data Analyst (for cross-checking trends).

- **Format:**
  - Short summary (Slack, email or document) with relevant tables and charts.

- **Includes:**
  - **Summary of failed / cancelled orders:**
    - Count by reason type (customer-side vs vendor-side vs system).
  - **Vendor-side issues:**
    - Top vendors and areas with SLA or behaviour issues.
    - Key incident examples (very short).
  - **Actions taken:**
    - Warnings.
    - Training recommendations.
    - Holds.
    - Closures.
  - **Immediate risks:**
    - Vendors or cities that need urgent attention (too many failures or complaints).
  - **Next week focus:**
    - Vendors and areas to monitor closely.
    - Any SOP or training updates planned.

### Monthly – "SLA & Vendor Quality Section in Monthly Review"

- **To:**
  - CEO.
  - Head of Operations.
  - Operations Manager.
  - Area & Serviceability.
  - CX Lead.
  - Data Analyst.
  - Sometimes Marketing (for impact on campaigns).

- **Format:**
  - Slides or document with charts and tables.

- **Includes:**
  - **Overall failure analysis:**
    - Total failed/cancelled orders and breakdown by reason type.
    - Trend vs previous month.
  - **Vendor SLA & quality performance:**
    - Vendors/associates grouped by performance (good/average/poor).
    - Cities and areas with recurring issues.
  - **Actions & outcomes:**
    - Number of warnings, holds, closures.
    - Impact of actions (before vs after patterns).
  - **Key patterns & root causes:**
    - Common issues (for example, particular time windows, devices, scripts, parts of process).
  - **Recommendations:**
    - Vendor and area cleanup.
    - Training topics.
    - Changes suggested in SOPs, flows or product.
