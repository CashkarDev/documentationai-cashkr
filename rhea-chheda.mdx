## Role & reporting line

- **Role:** Performance Marketing Specialist – Cashkr  
- **Department:** Marketing and Growth  
- **Reports to (primary):** Marketing Head  
- **Functional / dotted-line:**  
  - Data Analyst (for tracking, dashboards, experiment results)  
  - Head of Technology / IT Manager (for events, pixels, GA4, GTM, app and web tracking)  
- **Works closely with:**  
  - SEO and Content Team (blog and landing content alignment, search intent inputs)  
  - Graphic Designer / Video Editor (ad creatives based on briefs)  
  - UI/UX Designer (funnel and landing page improvement suggestions)  
  - Tech Team (tracking validation, custom events, deep links)  
  - CX / Sales Team (lead quality feedback and follow-through)  
  - Pricing and Buyback Manager (offers, price points, promo logic when needed)

## Role purpose

Plan, run and optimize paid campaigns (Google, Meta, YouTube and others) so that:

- Cashkr gets high-intent leads, orders and app installs.  
- Performance stays at or below target CPL, CPA and CPO with healthy ROAS where measurable.  
- Winning campaigns are scaled, weak campaigns are fixed or killed, and tracking remains accurate.

## Key result areas (KRAs)

- **KRA 1 – Performance strategy, goals and budget planning**  
  Own performance goals (leads, orders, installs, CPA, ROAS), select the right platforms and plan monthly and quarterly budget allocation across cities, device types and campaign types.

- **KRA 2 – Campaign setup, targeting and tracking readiness**  
  Ensure all campaigns are correctly structured and trackable: right objectives, targeting, UTMs, conversions and events (with implementation support from Tech).

- **KRA 3 – Ongoing optimization, testing and scaling**  
  Continuously optimize bids, budgets, keywords, audiences and creatives, while running A/B tests and scaling what works without breaking CPA and ROAS.

- **KRA 4 – Funnel and audience insights (analysis, not fixing)**  
  Analyse performance by funnel step, city, device, audience and creative; highlight where drop-offs happen and share specific recommendations with Tech, UX and Content.

- **KRA 5 – Reporting, communication and collaboration**  
  Provide clear daily, weekly and monthly performance updates and coordinate with Design, Tech, CX and Data without taking over their responsibilities.

## KPIs

### 1. Leads, orders and installs from paid channels

**Description:**  
Measures volume of leads, orders and app installs generated via paid campaigns (Google, Meta, YouTube and others).

**Formula:**

- Leads from paid campaigns per week and month  
- Orders from paid campaigns per week and month  
- App installs from paid campaigns per week and month

**Target:**  
Set monthly and quarterly targets by channel, city and device-type cluster.

**Data source:**  
Ads platforms (Google, Meta, UAC, P-Max, YouTube), GA4, Firebase, Admin Panel, Data Analyst dashboards.

**Review frequency:**  
Weekly and monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 1, KRA 3.

---

### 2. Cost per lead and cost per order

**Description:**  
Tracks average cost per lead and cost per order from paid campaigns versus target benchmarks.

**Formula:**

- CPL = Total spend ÷ leads from that channel or campaign  
- CPO or CPA = Total spend ÷ orders from that channel or campaign

**Target:**  
Stay at or below agreed CPL and CPO targets for each major channel, city and device segment.

**Data source:**  
Ads platforms, GA4, Admin Panel, dashboards.

**Review frequency:**  
Daily (quick check), weekly and monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 1, KRA 3.

---

### 3. ROAS and revenue efficiency

**Description:**  
For journeys where revenue is trackable, ensures campaigns drive profitable volume, not just volume.

**Formula:**  

- ROAS = Revenue attributed to campaign ÷ ad spend on that campaign

**Target:**  
Maintain or improve ROAS per channel and campaign as per agreed benchmarks.

**Data source:**  
GA4 revenue events, ads platforms, Admin Panel and Finance inputs, dashboards.

**Review frequency:**  
Monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 1, KRA 3.

---

### 4. CTR, CPC and CVR

**Description:**  
Core performance metrics to judge ad quality and funnel effectiveness.

**Formula:**

- CTR = Clicks ÷ impressions × 100  
- CPC = Spend ÷ clicks  
- CVR (click to lead or click to order) = Conversions ÷ clicks × 100

**Target:**  
Channel and campaign benchmarks that improve over time with testing.

**Data source:**  
Ads platforms, GA4 conversion events.

**Review frequency:**  
Daily (high-level) and weekly (deep dive).

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 2, KRA 3, KRA 4.

---

### 5. A/B test velocity and win rate

**Description:**  
Measures how actively performance marketing tests creatives, audiences and structures, and how often tests produce a winning variation.

**Formula (test count):**

- Number of meaningful A/B tests run per month (ad copy, creative, audience, bid strategy, landing page, etc.)

**Formula (win rate):**

- Number of tests that produce a clear winner (better CPA or ROAS) ÷ number of completed tests × 100

**Target:**  
Run a minimum number of tests per month (for example 4–8 or more, depending on scale) and maintain a strong learning rate, even if many tests fail.

**Data source:**  
Experiment log (Sheet or Notion), campaign change history, dashboards.

**Review frequency:**  
Monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 3, KRA 4.

---

### 6. Retargeting performance

**Description:**  
Effectiveness of retargeting campaigns (website visitors, app events, price viewers who did not convert).

**Metrics to track:**

- Share of total leads and orders coming from retargeting campaigns  
- CPL and CPO from retargeting vs cold campaigns  
- CVR of retargeting vs cold

**Target:**  
Retargeting should generally show better efficiency (lower CPL and CPO, higher CVR) than cold campaigns, with meaningful volume running through retargeting.

**Data source:**  
Ads platforms (retargeting campaign tags), GA4 audiences, pixels.

**Review frequency:**  
Weekly and monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 3, KRA 4.

---

### 7. Tracking and UTM implementation accuracy

**Description:**  
Checks whether all campaigns have correct UTMs, pixels and conversions attached. Performance Marketing verifies; Tech implements.

**Conceptual formula:**  

- Percentage of active campaigns or ad sets where:  
  - UTMs are correctly applied  
  - Correct conversion action is selected  
  - Relevant pixel or event is active

**Target:**  
100 percent of live campaigns should meet tracking and UTM standards.

**Data source:**  
Ads managers, GA4, tag checkers, UTM standard sheet.

**Review frequency:**  
Weekly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 2, KRA 5.

---

### 8. Lead quality feedback from CX and Sales

**Description:**  
Ensures performance marketing focuses on quality, not only quantity.

**Possible metrics:**

- Percentage of leads marked as junk, wrong or irrelevant by CX or Sales  
- Orders per 100 leads by campaign or audience  
- Qualitative feedback trends (for example bad device types, wrong locations)

**Target:**  
Maintain a low junk-lead percentage and improve conversion to orders using CX and Sales feedback.

**Data source:**  
CX and Sales tools, lead disposition data in Admin Panel, feedback sheets.

**Review frequency:**  
Monthly.

**Linked KRAs:**  
KRA 4, KRA 5.

## Core processes / SOPs owned

The Performance Marketing Specialist is owner or co-owner for these SOPs.

### Performance strategy and budget planning SOP

- Define goals for each month and quarter: leads, orders, installs, CPA and ROAS targets.  
- Decide platform mix (Google, Meta, YouTube and others) based on past performance and city or device priorities.  
- Allocate budgets across:  
  - Cities  
  - Device types  
  - Campaign types (cold, retargeting, branded, non-branded)

### Campaign and tracking setup SOP (with Tech and Data)

- Confirm required GA4 events and conversions exist (with Tech and Data).  
- Ensure pixels or SDKs are live (owned by Tech).  
- Define conversions inside each ad platform (leads, orders, installs).  
- Apply UTM standards for all campaigns.  
- Run a checklist before going live (naming, budget, location, schedule, conversion action, tracking).  
- Performance Marketer does not code or deploy GTM; they request and then verify implementation.

### Keyword and audience research SOP

- Research high-intent keywords for Google Search.  
- Group keywords into campaigns and ad groups by city, device and intent.  
- Build audiences in Meta and Google (interest, custom, lookalike, retargeting).  
- Refresh keywords and audiences on a defined cadence.

### Campaign creation and structure SOP

- Maintain naming conventions for campaigns, ad sets, ad groups and ads.  
- Decide when to use:  
  - Search, P-Max, Display, YouTube, Discovery (if used)  
  - Meta campaigns (Sales, Leads, Traffic and others)  
- Apply basic structural rules:  
  - Number of ads per ad group or ad set  
  - When to split by city or device value band  
  - Separation of cold vs retargeting campaigns

### Ad copy and creative briefing SOP

- Write ad structure: headlines, descriptions, primary text, CTAs.  
- Prepare creative briefs for designers and video editors: objective, angle or hook, formats, sizes, key messages, references.  
- Plan creative variations for A/B testing.  
- Performance Marketer writes and briefs; designers and video team produce assets.

### Daily optimization and A/B testing SOP

- Daily checks: spend, CPA, CPL, CTR, CPC, CVR, frequency.  
- Decide when to:  
  - Pause bad ads, ad sets or campaigns  
  - Add negative keywords  
  - Shift budgets between campaigns or ad sets  
  - Adjust bids or bid strategies  
- Define A/B testing rules:  
  - What to test (headline, creative, audience, bidding, placements)  
  - Minimum runtime and budget  
  - How to read and record results

### Retargeting and funnel-based campaigns SOP

- Build retargeting lists:  
  - Website visitors  
  - App events (for example viewed price, started order)  
- Configure time windows for retargeting audiences (for example 7, 14, 30 days).  
- Structure retargeting creatives and offers differently from cold campaigns.

### Competitor and market ads review SOP

- Regularly review competitor ads for creatives, messages, offers and landing styles.  
- Maintain a simple swipe file of interesting examples.  
- Translate learnings into new tests and creative or messaging angles.

### Performance reporting SOP

- **Daily snapshot:**  
  - Spend, leads, orders, installs, CPL, CPO or CPA and quick alerts.  
- **Weekly summary:**  
  - Channel-wise results, best and worst campaigns, tests run, next steps.  
- **Monthly report:**  
  - Deeper breakdown by city, device, campaign type and audience.  
  - Key insights and action items for the next month.

## Weekly & monthly reporting

### Weekly reporting

**Report:** Performance Marketing Weekly Snapshot  

**Primary audience:**

- Marketing Head  
- CEO (summary view)  
- Data Analyst  
- Tech Lead or IT Manager (when tracking issues exist)  
- Relevant Ops and CX stakeholders if needed for lead quality discussions

**Format:**  
Short summary (Slack, email or doc) with dashboard links.

**Includes:**

- **Core numbers:**  
  Spend, leads, orders, installs, CPL, CPO or CPA, ROAS (where available), with channel and campaign breakdown (Google, Meta, YouTube and others).  
- **What worked:**  
  Top performing campaigns, audiences, creatives, locations and device types.  
- **What did not work:**  
  High-CPL or low-conversion campaigns and corrective actions (pause, fix, test).  
- **A/B tests and learnings:**  
  Tests launched, completed and key learnings (including failed tests).  
- **Retargeting highlights:**  
  Performance of retargeting vs cold and new retargeting ideas.  
- **Next week plan:**  
  New tests, budget shifts, campaigns to scale or cut, and dependencies on Design, Tech, Data or CX.

### Monthly reporting

**Report:** Performance Marketing section in Monthly Review  

**Primary audience:**

- CEO  
- Marketing Head  
- Data Analyst  
- Tech Lead  
- CX Lead  
- Ops or Vendor Lead  
- Pricing and Buyback Manager (if relevant)

**Format:**  
Slides or document with charts and tables.

**Includes:**

- **Overall results:**  
  Leads, orders, installs, total spend, CPL, CPO and ROAS vs last month and vs targets.  
- **Breakdown views:**  
  - By channel (Google vs Meta vs YouTube and others)  
  - By city and device type  
  - By campaign type (cold vs retargeting, brand vs non-brand)  
- **Funnel and quality insights:**  
  CTR, CPC and CVR trends, plus integrated lead quality feedback from CX and Sales where available.  
- **Experiments and creative learnings:**  
  Biggest tests and outcomes, winning messages, audiences and formats.  
- **Tracking and tech notes:**  
  Major tracking issues and fixes, status of key conversion events.  
- **Top 3–5 priorities for next month:**  
  Campaigns, cities and devices to scale; weak campaigns to fix or replace; and any planned larger tests (new platforms, funnels or offers).
