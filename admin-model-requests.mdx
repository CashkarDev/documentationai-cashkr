## Overview

The Model Requests section shows device models that customers have asked Cashkr to support. Each entry is a customer who tried to sell a device, could not find it, and filled in the **Request a model** form.

Use this page to review incoming requests, add viable devices to the catalog, and close the loop with the customer.

<Callout kind="info">
Every row is a lost sale that can still be recovered. The customer has already told you they want to sell — they are only waiting on the catalog.
</Callout>

## What a request contains

Each request carries what the customer submitted:

- **Device type** — Selected from a dropdown, so this is reliable and routable.
- **Brand** — Free text as typed by the customer.
- **Model** — Free text as typed by the customer. Often carried over from what they searched for.
- **Requester** — The customer and their registered number, used for the notification.

<Callout kind="alert">
**Brand and model are unvalidated free text.** Expect misspellings, partial names, marketing names rather than model numbers, and occasional junk. Identify the real device before adding anything to the catalog — do not create a product from the raw string.
</Callout>

## Handling a request

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the actual device" icon="search">
    Work out which real device the customer means. Check the device type they selected, since that is the one constrained field and narrows the search considerably.

    Confirm the model genuinely is missing rather than listed under a different name, inactive, or missing pricing. A device already in the catalog but inactive needs reactivating, not recreating.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Decide whether to add it" icon="scale">
    Consider whether the device fits Cashkr's buyback range and whether vendors can realistically handle it. Requests for an entirely unsupported device type are a business decision, not a catalog task — escalate rather than close silently.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add the device with pricing and class" icon="plus">
    Create the device under the correct device type with its variants, base price, and an assigned class.

    All of these are required. See the warning below.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify it is actually searchable" icon="check-circle">
    Search for the model as a customer would. If it does not appear, it is not fulfilled, regardless of the record existing.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Notify the customer" icon="bell">
    Only after verifying, notify the requester on their registered number. The form promised them exactly this.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="alert">
**Do not notify before verifying.** Search returns only active products that have both pricing and a class. A device created without a base price or class will not appear, so a customer notified at that point returns, searches, finds nothing, and is worse off than if you had never contacted them.
</Callout>

## Prioritising

Requests are a direct demand signal — stronger than analytics, because the customer explicitly asked. When triaging:

- **Repeated requests for the same model** are the strongest case for adding a device. Watch for the same device arriving under different spellings.
- **Clusters by device type** may indicate a category gap worth a broader conversation.
- **A single obscure request** may not justify building out variants, pricing, and a class.

<Callout kind="info">
Because brand and model are free text, the same device can appear several ways. Scan for near-duplicates before concluding a model has only been asked for once.
</Callout>

## Connections to other modules

- **Devices** — Fulfilling a request means creating the device under the matching type in the [Devices](/phone) section.
- **Search Functionality** — The [search rules](/search-functionality) determine whether the added device actually becomes visible.
- **Classes** — A device without a [class](/classes) cannot be priced and will not surface.
- **Customer Notifications** — The closing notification goes out through the [customer notification](/customer-notification) channels.

## Open questions

<Callout kind="alert">
Several behaviours are **not yet confirmed**, including whether login is required to submit, which channel the notification uses, what happens when a request is declined, whether duplicate requests are merged, and whether the request is linked to any eventual order.

The full list is on [Model Requests](/model-requests). The decline path matters most operationally — the form makes an unconditional promise to notify, so a request that is quietly closed leaves that promise unmet.
</Callout>
