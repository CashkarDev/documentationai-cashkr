title: Reporting a Bug
description: What to include in a Cashkr bug report so it is actionable on first read.
---

Most rejected bug reports fail for the same reason: not enough identifiers to reproduce. This page is the minimum bar.

<Callout kind="tip">
Before filing, check [Order & lead issues](/help-center/troubleshooting/order-and-lead-issues) and [Pricing & coupon issues](/help-center/troubleshooting/pricing-and-coupon-issues). A large share of reports are documented business rules working as designed.
</Callout>

## Always include

<Steps>
  <Step title="Which surface" icon="layout-grid">
    Website, Customer App, Vendor App, Store App, or Admin Panel — plus app version and platform if mobile. The same backend behaves differently across surfaces and the fix location depends on this.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identifiers" icon="hash">
    Order ID, lead ID, customer phone, vendor ID, device variant — whichever apply. Without at least one, nobody can look at the actual record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Timestamp with timezone" icon="clock">
    Log correlation needs the time the action happened, not the time you filed the report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Expected versus actual" icon="git-compare">
    State both. "It is broken" is not reproducible. "Vendor with 40 credits could not unlock a Tier 2 phone in 400093" is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reproduction steps" icon="repeat">
    Numbered, from a clean session. Note whether it reproduces every time or intermittently.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence" icon="image">
    Screenshot or screen recording. For pricing issues, capture the full condition answer set, not just the final number.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Never include

<Callout kind="alert">
Do not paste **access tokens, JWTs, passwords, or full payment details** into a ticket, chat, or document.

If a token is genuinely needed to reproduce, say so and share it through whatever secret-sharing path your team uses — never in plain text in a ticket body. Tokens pasted into tickets outlive the ticket.
</Callout>

Also avoid pasting full customer records. An order ID is enough for anyone with access to pull the record themselves; a screenshot of personal data is not.

## Severity

| Level | Means | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Critical** | Money moves wrongly, or data is exposed | Wrong payout amount, credits lost without unlock, customer data visible to the wrong account |
| **High** | A core flow is blocked for many users | Sell flow broken in a city, vendors cannot unlock any orders, OTP not delivering |
| **Medium** | A flow is degraded or wrong for some users | Price mismatch on one device family, coupon rejected incorrectly |
| **Low** | Cosmetic or single-record | Label wrong, one order in an odd state |

Anything involving suspected fraud, a stolen device, or customer data exposure goes straight to trust and safety at critical, regardless of how many users it affects.

## Before you file

A quick self-check that saves a round trip:

- Does it reproduce on a second account or device?
- Does it reproduce on staging as well as production?
- Is there a documented rule that explains it? Search this site for the exact term first.
- Has it been reported already? Duplicate reports fragment the investigation.
