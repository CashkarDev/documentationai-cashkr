## Product categories at Cashkr

Understanding our product categories is key to how Cashkr evaluates, prices, and manages devices across the business.

<Callout kind="info">

Use this page whenever you need to confirm what Cashkr buys, which factors drive pricing, and what to check during inspection for each device category.

</Callout>

## Objective

- Train all employees on the main types of devices Cashkr buys.
- Explain valuation logic, inspection points, and key selling factors.
- Build confidence in assisting customers, vendors, and store partners accurately.

## Device categories

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Smartphones" icon="smartphone">

### Overview

Smartphones make up the majority of Cashkr customer orders and drive most day-to-day pricing conversations.

### Key valuation factors

- Physical condition: scratches, dents, display condition.
- Battery health and performance.
- Device age and whether the original box and accessories are available.
- IMEI status and security locks: unlocked, Find My iPhone turned off, no activation lock.

### Common issues

- Locked phones (IMEI or account locks).
- Cracked or heavily scratched screens.
- Activation locks such as iCloud or Google account lock.

### Inspection checklist

- Display: brightness, touch response, dead pixels, cracks.
- Battery: charge retention and abnormal heating.
- Camera: front and rear focus, clarity, flash.
- Audio: mic, speaker, and earpiece clarity.
- Body: frame, back panel, buttons, and ports.
- Sensors: fingerprint or face unlock, proximity, and other basic sensors.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Laptops" icon="monitor">

### Overview

Laptops are a growing category for Cashkr and are important for both individual customers and corporate buyback deals.

### Key valuation factors

- Processor: Intel i3, i5, i7, Apple silicon, or similar.
- RAM and storage: capacity in gigabytes or terabytes.
- Screen condition: scratches, dead pixels, discoloration.
- Battery health and performance under normal use.
- Physical damage: hinge stability, keyboard keys, trackpad, and body condition.

### Common issues

- Cracked or flickering screens.
- Loose or broken hinges.
- Missing keys or non-functional keyboard sections.
- Dead battery or device not powering on without charger.

### Inspection checklist

- Power on test: device should boot to operating system.
- Screen: brightness range, color, and dead pixels.
- Keyboard and trackpad: check all keys and basic gestures.
- Ports: charging, USB, HDMI, and any other major ports.
- Webcam and microphone: basic video and audio test.

### Special notes

- Corporate buybacks may involve multiple laptops in bulk. Confirm counts, configurations, and condition category clearly per batch.

</Tab>
<Tab title="MacBooks" icon="apple">

### Overview

MacBooks have unique inspection and pricing logic compared to Windows laptops, especially around Apple ecosystem locks.

### Key valuation factors

- Model: MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, and specific chip variants such as M1 or M2.
- Processor type: Intel versus Apple silicon.
- RAM, storage, and year of manufacture.
- Physical wear: body dents, keyboard wear, trackpad response.
- Screen issues: lamination problems, lines, or discoloration.

### Common issues

- Screen lamination or coating peel.
- Faulty or sticky keyboard keys.
- Battery with high cycle count or poor backup.

### Inspection checklist

- Serial number and basic model verification.
- Screen quality and brightness.
- Keyboard and trackpad responsiveness.
- Battery cycle count and health in system settings.

### Special notes

- Find My Mac and Apple ID lock must be turned off before pickup.
- Confirm that the customer has signed out of iCloud and any remote lock features.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Tablets & iPads" icon="tablet">

### Overview

Tablets, including iPads, contribute to both consumer orders and some B2B or institutional deals.

### Key valuation factors

- Display condition: scratches, cracks, touch responsiveness.
- Battery health and backup under normal use.
- Functional components: touch, microphone, speaker, and buttons.
- Connectivity options: Wi‑Fi only or Wi‑Fi plus cellular variants.

### Common issues

- Cracked or heavily scratched screens.
- Locked or activation-linked devices.
- Missing chargers or accessories.

### Inspection checklist

- Display and touch: scroll, multi-touch, and edge responsiveness.
- Audio: speaker output and microphone input.
- Buttons and ports: power, volume, home button if present, and charging port.
- Connectivity: Wi‑Fi connection and cellular network where applicable.

### Special notes

- For iPads, Find My iPad must be turned off before sale and the device must be removed from the customer account.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## General product insights

- **Accessories and box:** Original accessories and the original box usually add around 5–10 percent to the resale value.
- **Device age:** Older models depreciate faster, while recently launched models retain higher value.
- **Market demand:** Pricing adjusts dynamically based on current demand and supply for each model.
- **Unsellable devices:** Devices that are too old or no longer supported are marked as not supported and are not accepted.

## Example: value difference by condition (illustrative)

- **Typical resale range:** Around 50–60 percent of original maximum retail price.

<Tab title="Fair / Average">

<Tab title="Faulty">

- **Typical resale range:** Below 25 percent of original maximum retail price.
- **Example:** Device not booting, display not working, or other major hardware faults.
