## Overview

**Used by**

- Store Partnerships Team  
- Vendor Management Team  
- Operations Managers and Associates involved in store vendor oversight

**Purpose**

Train internal teams on how to explain and enforce SLA (Service Level Agreement) rules for B2Ch store vendors, covering timeliness, compliance, and behavior expectations.

**Objective**

- Define clear SLA expectations for store-based vendors  
- Identify and classify SLA violations  
- Follow a consistent communication and penalty structure  
- Maintain vendor discipline while ensuring fair review  

## Training script (for vendor managers)

<Steps>

<Step title="1. What SLA means in the B2Ch store vendor model" icon="info">

> "SLA stands for Service Level Agreement, which defines the expected performance and behavioral standards every active store vendor must follow.  
>  
> These standards ensure that all stores receive smooth service, timely collections, and accurate payouts — maintaining brand trust and operational quality."

</Step>

<Step title="2. Store vendor SLA components" icon="settings">

Use this table to explain the main SLA components, how they work, and what counts as a violation.

| SLA category        | Definition                                                         | Example of violation                             |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Timeliness          | Orders must be created, verified, and completed within the same working day. | Device inspection delayed beyond 30 minutes.     |
| Payment compliance  | Customer must receive payout or voucher immediately after ID verification. | Payment pending for more than 15 minutes.        |
| Collection SLA      | All devices must be collected from store before closing.          | Paid orders not collected the same day.          |
| Reporting SLA       | End-of-day report submission via app by 7:30 PM.                  | No summary submitted.                            |
| Behavioral SLA      | Maintain professionalism and proper communication at store.       | Argument with store staff or customers.          |

</Step>

<Step title="3. How SLA is monitored" icon="monitor">

> "All SLAs are tracked automatically through the Vendor App and the Ops Dashboard.  
>  
> Order timestamps track pickup and payment speed.  
> App sync ensures collection and reporting completion.  
> Customer and store complaints are verified through call logs or photos.  
>  
> Every SLA metric generates a daily compliance score visible in the vendor’s dashboard."

</Step>

<Step title="4. SLA score breakdown and observation list" icon="bar-chart">

Use this breakdown to explain how overall SLA score is calculated.

| Component                       | Weightage | Description                               |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Order completion (same day)    | 40%       | Must close all orders before store closing. |
| Payment timeliness             | 30%       | Instant transfer or voucher issue.        |
| Reporting accuracy             | 20%       | End-of-day summary submission.            |
| Professional conduct           | 10%       | No complaints or policy breaches.         |

<Callout kind="info">

If a vendor’s overall SLA score falls below 80%, the vendor enters the Observation List.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step title="5. Common SLA violations and severity" icon="alert-triangle">

Use this table to classify violations when you review vendor performance.

| Violation type            | Description                                        | Severity |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Delayed order processing  | Orders left incomplete beyond same-day deadline.  | Medium   |
| Payment delay             | Wallet payment or voucher not issued on time.     | High     |
| Missed collection         | Device left uncollected overnight.                | High     |
| No daily report           | Missed end-of-day summary.                        | Medium   |
| Misbehavior               | Rude or unprofessional tone with store or customer. | High  |
| App manipulation          | Fake order marking, manual pricing misuse.        | Critical |

</Step>

<Step title="6. Violation escalation and action policy" icon="shield">

Use this escalation ladder whenever you take action on SLA violations.

| Step                                | Description                        | Action                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| First violation                     | Verbal warning and note in log.   | Friendly reminder via call or app notification. |
| Second violation                    | Written warning.                  | SLA warning slip sent via email or Slack.      |
| Third violation (within 7 days)     | Temporary hold.                   | Vendor paused for 24–48 hours.                 |
| Repeated (5 or more weekly)         | Operations escalation.            | Report to Ops Manager for review.              |
| Severe (fraud, abuse, manipulation) | Immediate suspension.             | Direct deactivation and audit review.          |

<Callout kind="alert">

Log all actions under the Vendor SLA Tracker Sheet with timestamp and violation type.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step title="7. Communication template for violations (internal use)" icon="users">

Use this script when notifying a vendor of an SLA violation.

> "Hi [Vendor Name],  
>  
> During today’s review, we noticed a delay or missed action on order [Order ID].  
>  
> As per SLA policy, this is marked as a [Violation Type]. Please ensure compliance in future.  
>  
> Repeated issues may lead to Hold status or performance review.  
>  
> Regards,  
> Cashkr Ops Team."

</Step>

<Step title="8. Appeal or clarification flow" icon="help-circle">

> "Vendors can appeal a violation within 24 hours by submitting clarification or proof such as a screenshot or call log.  
>  
> The Vendor Management Team reviews it with the Ops Manager.  
> If valid, the violation is marked ‘Cleared’ and no penalty applies.  
> Otherwise, it remains recorded for SLA history tracking."

<Callout kind="alert">

Respect the 24-hour appeal window. After 24 hours, keep the violation recorded in SLA history unless a manager explicitly decides otherwise.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step title="9. Why SLA enforcement is important" icon="lock">

> "Strict SLA enforcement ensures:  
> • Customers always receive timely service.  
> • Stores maintain trust in Cashkr vendors.  
> • Vendor performance remains measurable.  
> • Fraud and delays are minimized.  
>  
> It’s not just about punishment — it’s about standardizing quality."

</Step>

<Step title="10. Internal reminders for vendor managers" icon="terminal">

Use this table as an internal checklist for ongoing SLA monitoring and enforcement.

| Task              | Frequency | Owner            |
| ----------------- | --------- | ---------------- |
| SLA score review  | Daily     | Vendor Manager   |
| Violation log update | Real-time | Ops Associate |
| Hold review       | Weekly    | Ops Manager      |
| Policy audit      | Monthly   | Compliance Team  |

</Step>

</Steps>

## Key talking points

- SLA equals timeliness, compliance, and conduct.  
- All metrics are automatically tracked — manual excuses are not accepted.  
- Vendors must stay above 80% SLA weekly to remain active.  
- Repeated violations trigger Hold, not instant removal — fairness first.  
- Communication with vendors must remain professional, never confrontational.
