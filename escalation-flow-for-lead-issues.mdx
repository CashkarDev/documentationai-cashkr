## Overview

Use this escalation flow when a lead is blocked and you cannot move it forward yourself.

- **Who it is for:** Inbound Sales, Follow-Up, CX, Ops, BD, and Management teams.
- **What it covers:** When to escalate, who to escalate to, SLAs, and how to document and track escalations.
- **Outcome:** No high-intent lead is lost due to internal delays, confusion, or missing ownership.

<Callout kind="info">

If a lead is stuck, escalate. Do not wait.

</Callout>

## Purpose

Use this SOP to:

- Resolve lead issues quickly
- Avoid losing high-intent leads
- Assign the correct owning team
- Maintain clear accountability
- Ensure proper documentation for audits

## What is a lead escalation?

A lead escalation happens when a Sales or CX agent cannot move a lead forward due to a blocker that is not resolvable at agent level.

Typical reasons include:

- System limitation
- Coverage issue
- Pricing conflict
- Operational dependency
- Customer complaint or CX risk

Escalation ensures the right team intervenes immediately.

## Common lead issues that require escalation

| Issue type     | Example                                |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Serviceability | Pincode not available but demand is high |
| Pricing        | Customer has a confirmed competitor quote |
| Technical      | Model missing or price not loading    |
| Pickup         | Vendor unavailable for a high-intent lead |
| Coupon         | Coupon not applying correctly         |
| Data           | Lead assigned incorrectly or duplicate lead |
| CX risk        | Customer is upset and complaint is likely |

## Escalation ownership matrix

| Issue                  | Escalate to          | SLA       |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | --------- |
| Pincode or area issue  | BD team              | Same day  |
| Vendor availability    | Ops or Vendor Ops    | Same day  |
| Pricing or match request | Ops or Pricing team | Same day  |
| App or website bug     | Tech and CX          | Immediate |
| Coupon issue           | Marketing or Tech    | Same day  |
| Lead data issue        | Ops or Admin         | Same day  |
| Customer anger         | CX lead or Ops       | Immediate |

## Step-by-step escalation flow

<Steps>

<Step title="Identify the blocker" icon="alert-triangle">

- Confirm the lead cannot move forward.
- Confirm the issue cannot be resolved at agent level.

</Step>

<Step title="Capture lead details" icon="edit">

Collect these mandatory details before escalating:

- Lead ID
- Customer name and number
- Device and city
- Issue summary
- Impact (conversion risk, high-end device, or similar)

</Step>

<Step title="Escalate via approved channel" icon="arrow-right">

- Use the defined internal channel (for example, Slack, internal tool, or WhatsApp group).
- Use a clear subject line so the receiver understands urgency and context.

**Subject line example:**

```text
Escalation | High-intent lead | Pickup not available | Mumbai
```

</Step>

<Step title="Update lead notes" icon="edit">

Add notes in the Admin Panel so anyone can see the escalation context.

**Admin Panel note example:**

```text
Escalated to BD – pincode not serviceable.
High-intent lead.
Awaiting confirmation.
```

</Step>

<Step title="Follow up and re-escalate if needed" icon="repeat">

- Track the response on the escalation channel.
- If there is no response within the SLA, re-escalate to the next level (team lead, then management).

</Step>

</Steps>

## Escalation levels

Use these levels to push unresolved issues higher when needed.

| Level  | Who                    | When to use                                  |
| ------ | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Level 1 | Functional team        | First resolution attempt (BD, Ops, Tech, Marketing) |
| Level 2 | Team lead or manager   | When there is delay or no action at Level 1 |
| Level 3 | Management             | High-value leads, CX escalation risk, or repeated unresolved issues |

## Handling high-value or high-end leads

<Callout kind="alert">

For iPhone, MacBook, and other premium or high-value devices:

- Escalate immediately.
- Mark the lead as **High priority**.
- Assign a senior agent to handle follow-ups.
- Track the lead closely until closure.

High-end leads must never wait silently.

</Callout>

## Customer communication during escalation

<Callout kind="tip">

Use a clear holding line and manage expectations professionally.

**Approved holding line**

> I am checking this with our team and will update you shortly. Thank you for your patience.

**Do**

- Acknowledge the issue.
- Set the expectation that you will update the customer.
- Follow up and close the loop.

**Do not**

- Blame internal teams.
- Promise timelines you cannot control.
- Ignore the customer while the escalation is open.

</Callout>

## When not to escalate

Avoid raising escalations for normal lead behavior or when there is no real blocker:

- Customer is thinking or comparing options.
- Customer has requested a callback later.
- Lead is confirmed as not serviceable and the customer has been informed.
- Customer is clearly not interested or has declined the offer.

Escalations are for blockers, not for delays by customer choice.

## Escalation tracking and daily review

Run a daily review of escalations to maintain quality and identify patterns:

- Total number of escalations raised
- Average and maximum resolution time
- Conversion outcome for escalated leads
- Repeated issues by type (serviceability, pricing, tech, etc.)

Use these insights across Ops, Sales leads, and Management to fix root causes and improve SLAs.

## Common escalation mistakes to avoid

- Escalating without required lead details
- Not adding Admin Panel notes
- Escalating non-issues or low-intent leads
- Waiting too long before escalating a true blocker
- Not following up or re-escalating after the SLA is breached
